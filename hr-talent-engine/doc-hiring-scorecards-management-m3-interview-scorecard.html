Level Context: What M3 Looks Like

M3 is a senior HR leader responsible for a significant portion of or the entire HR function, managing M1 and M2 managers and shaping how HR operates at organizational scale. Evaluate for:

  • Manager development — Develops M1 and M2 HR managers into excellent people leaders
  • HR function excellence — Establishes standards, practices, and culture across all five HR disciplines
  • Strategic leadership — Aligns HR strategy with company objectives and contributes to executive discussions
  • Organizational health — Builds a scalable, healthy HR function that enables business performance

Differentiation from M2: M3 shapes the HR function and develops other managers; M2 manages teams and programs independently. Differentiation from E1: M3 leads the HR function operationally; E1 owns strategic HR domains as an executive.

Competency Assessment

1. Manager Development & Organizational Leadership

What to assess: Ability to develop M1 and M2 managers, build bench strength, and create a management culture within HR.

M3 Expectations: - Develops M1 and M2 HR managers into excellent people leaders—not just effective practitioners who happen to manage - Delegates to develop managers while maintaining accountability for outcomes across all HR disciplines - Creates a management culture with shared practices, standards, and expectations that reflect the rigor HR asks of the organization - Builds bench strength and succession planning so the HR function is not dependent on any single manager

Sample Questions: - Tell me about M1 or M2 managers you've developed. What was your approach and what did their growth look like? - How do you delegate in a way that develops managers rather than just distributing tasks? - How have you built a management culture within your HR organization? What practices and standards did you establish? - Describe your approach to succession planning within the HR function. How do you identify and develop future leaders?

2. HR Function Excellence & Cross-Discipline Integration

What to assess: Ability to establish standards across all five HR disciplines and create an integrated, scalable HR function.

M3 Expectations: - Establishes HR standards and practices that measurably improve service quality, employee experience, compliance, and operational efficiency - Creates cross-discipline integration—recruiting feeding into onboarding, onboarding connecting to L&D, HRBP insights informing compensation strategy - Builds an HR function that scales effectively with company growth without proportional increase in headcount or coordination overhead - Attracts and retains strong HR talent, creating a function known for the quality of its people and impact of its work

Sample Questions: - How have you built an integrated HR function where disciplines work as a system rather than in silos? - Tell me about HR standards or practices you established. How did you drive adoption and measure impact? - Describe how you scaled an HR function through company growth. What worked and what would you do differently? - How do you attract and retain strong HR talent? What makes your HR organization a place people want to work?

3. Strategic Leadership & Business Alignment

What to assess: Strategic thinking, ability to align HR strategy with company objectives, and contribution to executive discussions.

M3 Expectations: - Aligns HR strategy with company objectives, with measurable progress and regular executive visibility - Plans forward 6-18 months—anticipating workforce planning needs, capability gaps, compliance changes, and technology investments - Builds cross-functional partnerships with Finance, Legal, IT, and business leadership that position HR as a strategic partner - Contributes meaningfully to company strategy, representing the people perspective in executive discussions

Sample Questions: - Walk me through how you've developed and executed an HR strategy aligned with company objectives. - How do you anticipate organizational needs 6-18 months ahead? Give me a specific example. - Tell me about a cross-functional partnership you built that positioned HR as a strategic partner. What did it look like? - Describe a time you contributed to company strategy from the people perspective. What was the situation and your impact?

4. Culture, Compliance & Organizational Health

What to assess: Ability to shape company culture through HR programs, maintain compliance posture, and sustain organizational health at scale.

M3 Expectations: - Shapes company-wide people culture deliberately through HR programs, policies, and practices - Maintains robust compliance posture across all HR domains, appropriate to the organization's size, industry, and geography - Ensures employment practices are fair, consistent, and legally defensible, with judgment about when flexibility serves the organization - Builds an inclusive culture within the HR function and monitors organizational health proactively

Sample Questions: - How have you deliberately shaped organizational culture through HR programs and practices? What worked? - Tell me about a compliance challenge you navigated. How did you balance risk management with business needs? - Describe how you've maintained organizational health across your HR teams, particularly through demanding business cycles. - How do you handle the tension between consistency in employment practices and context-appropriate flexibility?

5. Executive Communication & Systems Thinking

What to assess: Executive presence, ability to communicate with C-level leadership, and systems thinking across the HR function.

M3 Expectations: - Communicates effectively with C-level leadership and board when needed, representing the people agenda with authority and nuance - Articulates HR vision, strategy, and priorities clearly at all organizational levels - Understands how HR disciplines, business cycles, organizational dynamics, and people decisions interact across a complex organization - Makes sound decisions under ambiguity when information is incomplete, stakes are high, and multiple legitimate interests are in tension

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a time you presented HR strategy or a people initiative to executive leadership. How did you frame it? - How do you think about the interdependencies across HR disciplines, business cycles, and organizational dynamics? - Describe a high-stakes decision you made with incomplete information. What was your reasoning process? - How do you navigate competing priorities between different stakeholders—executive team, managers, employees, and the HR team itself?
