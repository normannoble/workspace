Level Context: What E2 Looks Like

E2 is a senior executive who operates across multiple HR domains or owns the day-to-day leadership of the entire HR function. Evaluate for:

  • Enterprise HR leadership — Ensures all of HR works together effectively and delivers a coherent employee experience
  • Executive partnership — Operates as a true partner to the CPO and C-suite, not just a functional leader reporting up
  • Developing executives — Grows Directors (E1s) toward Senior Director and CPO-level capability
  • Organizational transformation — Positions HR as a driver of transformation, not just a support function

Differentiation from E1: E2 leads across multiple HR domains with enterprise perspective; E1 owns a specific domain. Differentiation from E3: E2 partners with and deputizes for the CPO; E3 owns the people vision and represents it to the board.

Competency Assessment

1. Cross-Domain HR Leadership

What to assess: Ability to lead across all HR domains, ensuring strategic coherence and a seamless employee experience.

E2 Expectations: - Multiple HR domains work together effectively, delivering a coherent and seamless employee experience - Organizational effectiveness across the HR function—coordination, prioritization, and resource allocation that optimizes for company outcomes - Strategic coherence across all people programs—investments in hiring, development, compensation, and culture tell a consistent story - Cross-domain problems resolved with lasting solutions that don't require ongoing senior intervention

Sample Questions: - How have you ensured strategic coherence across multiple HR domains? Give me a specific example. - Describe a cross-domain problem you resolved. What was the lasting solution and how did it hold up? - How do you prioritize and allocate resources across HR disciplines when everything feels critical? - Walk me through your approach to creating a seamless employee experience across the full talent lifecycle.

2. Executive Partnership & Board Engagement

What to assess: Ability to operate as a C-suite peer, partner with the CPO, contribute to enterprise strategy, and engage with the board.

E2 Expectations: - Operates as a true partner to the CPO—contributes to enterprise people strategy, not just executes on it - Represents the CPO and the HR function credibly in executive forums, board contexts, or external settings - Shapes company strategy through active contribution, with people perspective integrated into all major business decisions - Builds cross-functional executive relationships at the C-suite level that drive company-level outcomes

3. Developing Executive Leaders

What to assess: Ability to develop Directors (E1s) toward Senior Director and CPO-level capability, and build executive bench.

E2 Expectations: - Develops Directors (E1s) toward Senior Director or CPO-level capability through deliberate development and increasing enterprise exposure - Builds an executive leadership culture within HR that models excellence for the broader organization - Ensures succession planning at the executive level—the HR function isn't dependent on any single leader - Creates a leadership bench that provides optionality for the company's future people leadership needs

How did you grow their enterprise thinking and executive capability? - How do you approach succession planning at the executive level within HR? - Describe the executive leadership culture you've built within your HR organization. 4. Organizational Transformation & Enterprise Strategy

What to assess: Ability to drive organizational transformation, develop multi-year people strategy, and position HR as a competitive advantage.

E2 Expectations: - Positions HR as a driver of organizational transformation, not just a support function responding to business needs - Leads enterprise-wide change initiatives effectively—cultural shifts, organizational redesigns, and workforce transformations with lasting results - Develops people strategy that positions the company competitively over 2-5 year horizons - Ensures board and investor confidence in the company's people strategy, talent pipeline, and organizational health

Sample Questions: - Tell me about an organizational transformation you drove through the people function. What was the scope and lasting impact? - How do you develop people strategy with a 2-5 year horizon? Walk me through your approach with a real example. - Describe how you've positioned HR as a driver of business transformation, not just a support function. - How have you built board or investor confidence in the company's people strategy and organizational health?

5. External Presence & Industry Leadership

What to assess: Executive presence with external audiences, thought leadership, and ability to build the company's employer brand and HR reputation.

E2 Expectations: - Represents the company's people capabilities credibly to external audiences—candidates, customers, partners, investors, and industry - Builds thought leadership that strengthens the company's employer brand and reputation as a talent destination - Cultivates industry relationships that create strategic value—benchmarking networks, HR leadership communities, and talent market intelligence - Contributes to the broader HR community in ways that reflect well on the organization

Sample Questions: - How have you represented your company's people story to external audiences? Give me a specific example. - Describe your approach to building the company's employer brand and external reputation through people leadership. - What industry relationships have you cultivated and how have they created strategic value for your organization? - How do you stay ahead of workforce trends and bring external perspective into your people strategy?
