Executive Track Offboarding — Human Resources

Overview

Track: Executive Function: Human Resources Levels Covered: E1 (Director of HR) through E3 (Chief People Officer)

Offboarding an HR executive is a strategic organizational event. These leaders own the company's people strategy, shape organizational culture, maintain board-level relationships, and hold the most sensitive knowledge about the company's workforce — compensation structures, succession plans, organizational health assessments, pending restructurings, and confidential personnel matters at the most senior levels. Their departure creates risk across multiple dimensions: strategic continuity, stakeholder confidence, organizational stability, and competitive exposure.

The complexity and visibility of executive offboarding increases dramatically with level. An E1 departure requires domain strategy preservation and senior leader transitions. An E3 departure is a company-level event requiring board notification, organizational communication, external narrative management, and potentially months of structured succession and transition planning. Because HR executives have the most comprehensive view of the company's people landscape — including information that could create competitive or legal risk if mishandled — offboarding must balance thoroughness with appropriate urgency on information security and access management.

Departure Type Handling

Voluntary — Resignation

Notice periods by level:

Level Standard Notice Recommended Minimum Priority Focus
E1 4-6 weeks 6 weeks Domain strategy preservation, senior leader transitions, M3 development continuity
E2 6-8 weeks 8 weeks Cross-domain coordination, executive partnership transition, organizational transformation continuity
E3 8-12 weeks 12 weeks Board notification, company-wide communication, complete strategic transition, executive search initiation

Standard process: 1. Confirm last day with executive leadership (CPO for E1-E2; CEO/Board for E3) 2. Immediately assess strategic impact and identify interim leadership 3. Develop comprehensive transition plan covering: people strategy, organizational knowledge, executive relationships, board context, and sensitive matters 4. For E2-E3: initiate executive search process immediately — these roles take months to fill 5. For E3: coordinate board notification, organizational communication, and external narrative 6. Execute structured knowledge transfer with designated successor or interim leader 7. Transition external relationships: investors, partners, industry contacts, advisory boards

Voluntary — Retirement

Retirement of HR executives offers the best possible transition scenario — extended timeline, willing participant, and opportunity for deliberate successor development.

  • Extended transition: For E1, negotiate 3-4 months; for E2, 4-6 months; for E3, 6-12 months if possible. The investment in a longer transition pays off dramatically compared to a rushed handover
  • Successor development: If an internal successor is identified, create a structured transition plan with graduated responsibility transfer — the executive and successor co-lead for a defined period, with the executive shifting to an advisory role over time
  • Strategic documentation: Commission comprehensive strategic documents: "state of the people function" for E2-E3, domain strategy and assessment for E1, organizational health intelligence, and forward-looking strategic recommendations
  • Relationship transition: Execute relationship transitions personally over months, not days — board members, investors, executive peers, and external partners should meet the successor multiple times before the departure
  • Advisory arrangement: E1-E3 retirees may serve as strategic advisors post-departure; establish terms before departure (4-8 hours/month for 6-12 months). This is especially valuable for E3 departures where complete knowledge transfer is impossible within any notice period
  • Celebration and legacy: Acknowledge the executive's contributions at a level appropriate to their impact — board recognition, company-wide acknowledgment, and documentation of their strategic contributions for organizational history

Involuntary — Performance-Based

Performance-based terminations at the executive level are rare, high-stakes events that require meticulous preparation and execution.

  • Pre-termination preparation: Before the termination conversation, the following must be complete: interim leadership identified and briefed, legal review of termination terms, communication plan drafted for all audiences, strategic knowledge preservation assessed, and security/access revocation plan ready
  • Legal considerations: Executive terminations typically involve negotiated separation agreements, non-compete and non-solicitation provisions, and careful handling of any claims. Legal counsel must be involved from the earliest planning stages
  • Knowledge transfer limitations: The departing executive may have limited willingness or availability for knowledge transfer. The organization must have already reduced single-executive-failure risk during the performance management process
  • Immediate priorities: Secure all sensitive documents, ensure HRIS and employee data access is revoked appropriately, and immediately establish interim leadership with authority to act
  • Communication: Move quickly on communication to direct reports, the HR function, executive team, and (for E3) the board. Silence breeds speculation and erodes confidence
  • See Underperformance Management Guide

Involuntary — Layoff

Layoffs affecting HR executives are unusual but may occur during major restructurings, M&A integrations, or strategic pivots.

  • Organizational impact: Executive layoffs signal significant organizational change. The communication plan must address not just the departure but the organizational direction it implies
  • Severance and transition: Executive severance packages should reflect both the level and the sensitive organizational knowledge the executive carries. Consider extended non-compete provisions and consulting arrangements that keep the executive engaged during the transition
  • Remaining leadership: Address the impact on the remaining HR leadership team directly. Executive departures create uncertainty, and the remaining leaders need clarity on structure, strategy, and their own futures
  • External narrative: For E2-E3, coordinate external messaging with the communications team. The departure will be visible to investors, customers, partners, and candidates — get ahead of the narrative
  • Board communication (E3): Immediate board notification with a clear plan for interim leadership and the search process

Knowledge Transfer Plan

E1 — Director of HR

Key knowledge areas: - Domain strategy: The 1-3 year strategic vision for their HR domain, including the reasoning behind strategic bets, program investments, and organizational design decisions - Business value connection: How domain programs create measurable business value — the metrics, the causal logic, and the evidence base that justifies people investments to the business - Senior leader development: M3 development trajectories, succession readiness assessments, and the E1's assessment of leadership bench strength within the domain - Executive relationships: Cross-functional partnerships with other Directors, VPs, and C-level executives, including context on joint initiatives, negotiated agreements, and relationship dynamics - Program portfolio: All major programs within the domain — strategy, ownership, metrics, maturity, and planned evolution - Organizational health: Engagement and retention intelligence within the domain, including known risks, cultural dynamics, and upcoming challenges - Budget and investments: Domain budget, vendor contracts, planned investments, and resource allocation rationale - External relationships: Industry contacts, vendor executive relationships, advisory board connections, and professional community involvement

Transfer method: Structured daily sessions with successor/interim for 2-3 weeks; written domain strategy and assessment document; executive transition meetings with CPO and cross-functional executives; M3 development briefings; program portfolio handoff to domain leadership team

Estimated transfer time: 3-5 weeks

Level-specific considerations: - Document the strategic rationale behind every significant domain decision made in the last 12-24 months — the "what" is usually visible, but the "why" and "what we considered but rejected" are not - Transfer M3 development context personally — this is uniquely valuable knowledge that directly affects leadership succession - Introduce successor to cross-functional executive partners in joint meetings, providing context on shared initiatives and relationship dynamics - Create a "domain assessment and forward plan" document that serves as the successor's strategic foundation - Transition external relationships (vendors, industry contacts) with personal introductions - Ensure board-relevant context (for domains with board reporting) transfers completely

E2 — Senior Director of HR

Key knowledge areas: - Enterprise people strategy: The cross-domain strategic vision for the HR function, including how all domains integrate to deliver a coherent employee experience and competitive advantage through people - CPO partnership context: How the E2 partners with the CPO — decision-making dynamics, delegated authority, strategic priorities, and the working relationship that enables the function - Executive leadership development: E1 development trajectories, succession readiness assessments, and the E2's assessment of the executive HR bench - C-suite relationships: Partnerships with the CFO, CTO, and other C-level executives, including context on strategic alignment, joint initiatives, and the political dynamics of executive decision-making - Organizational transformation context: In-flight organizational changes, cultural shifts, and workforce transformations, including the strategic reasoning and expected outcomes - Board and investor context: People strategy narratives, talent metrics, and organizational health frameworks used for board and investor communication (if applicable) - External presence: Industry relationships, thought leadership commitments, and external representation of the company's people capabilities - Organizational intelligence: The E2's deep understanding of organizational health, succession risks, retention threats, cultural dynamics, and the strategic talent landscape across the company - Function-wide operations: How the HR function is structured, how cross-domain coordination works, and the operational model that enables HR delivery at scale

Transfer method: Extended knowledge transfer program with successor/interim: dedicated daily sessions for the first 2-3 weeks, then transitioning to regular check-ins; comprehensive written strategic documents; executive transition meetings with CPO, CEO, and C-suite peers; board-level briefing handoff (if applicable); E1 development briefings; external relationship transitions

Estimated transfer time: 4-8 weeks

Level-specific considerations: - The E2 often knows things the CPO doesn't — operational details, team dynamics, and relationship context that exist at the E2 level. Capture this in written form - Document the CPO partnership model explicitly: what decisions the E2 makes independently, what requires CPO input, and how they coordinate on enterprise strategy - Transfer E1 development context with the same care as the M3-level handoff — these are the company's future senior HR leaders - Transition C-suite relationships personally, with particular attention to the CFO partnership (which affects HR budget, workforce planning, and compensation decisions) - Create a "state of the HR function" document that captures: current structure, strategic priorities, organizational health assessment, key risks, talent pipeline status, and recommended investments - For external relationships, prioritize industry connections that create strategic value for the company — benchmarking networks, talent market intelligence sources, and conference commitments - Coordinate with the CPO on how the E2's responsibilities will be distributed during the interim period — this affects the entire HR leadership structure

E3 — Chief People Officer

Key knowledge areas: - People vision and strategy: The company-level people strategy and how it creates sustainable competitive advantage — talent acquisition approach, culture architecture, compensation philosophy, leadership development strategy, and organizational design principles - Board and investor context: Board relationships, people strategy narratives, talent risk reporting frameworks, succession planning presentations, and the trust and credibility built through board interactions over time - CEO partnership: The working relationship with the CEO on people matters — how decisions are made, what the CEO values in people counsel, and the dynamics of executive alignment on organizational matters - C-suite partnerships: Relationships with every executive, including context on how people strategy intersects with their functions, ongoing negotiations, and the political landscape of executive decision-making - Organizational intelligence: The most comprehensive view of the company's people landscape — retention risks, succession gaps, cultural strengths and vulnerabilities, compensation competitiveness, and organizational health across every function - Executive bench assessment: Candid assessments of the entire senior leadership team — strengths, risks, development needs, and succession readiness for every executive role - HR leadership development: E1 and E2 development trajectories, succession plans for the CPO role itself, and the CPO's assessment of the HR function's leadership capability - Sensitive matters: Pending or potential legal matters, executive compensation details, confidential restructuring plans, board-level personnel discussions, and any investigative matters at the senior level - External relationships: Investor contacts, board member relationships, industry thought leadership commitments, advisory board positions, and public commitments on behalf of the company - M&A context: Due diligence methodologies, integration playbooks, and any active or anticipated M&A-related people work - Institutional memory: Why major people and culture decisions were made, what alternatives were rejected, and what the organization learned from past approaches — the narrative of the company's people journey

Transfer method: Extended transition program (8-12 weeks minimum for planned departures): daily working sessions with successor/interim for weeks 1-4, transitioning to advisory cadence; comprehensive written strategic documents; board presentation on transition (joint with successor); CEO transition meetings (multiple sessions); C-suite partner transitions; investor communication transition (if applicable); HR leadership team briefings; external relationship introductions; recorded strategic perspectives for future reference

Estimated transfer time: 8-16 weeks (with advisory arrangement extending 6-12 months post-departure)

Level-specific considerations: - E3 departures are company-defining events. The transition affects organizational confidence, employer brand, talent retention, and external perception. Treat accordingly - No single person can absorb all of a CPO's knowledge. The transfer should be distributed: enterprise strategy to successor/interim, board context to CEO and successor jointly, executive assessments to CEO and board, domain-specific knowledge to E1s and E2s - Board transition: The departing CPO should present the transition plan to the board, introduce the successor in a board setting, and provide a comprehensive briefing document on people strategy, organizational health, and talent risks - CEO transition: Schedule multiple dedicated sessions between the departing CPO and CEO, covering: the working relationship model, how to evaluate people strategy effectiveness, key personnel decisions in progress, and the CPO's candid assessment of organizational health and leadership strength - External narrative: Work with communications to craft the departure announcement. How the company handles a CPO transition signals its organizational maturity and employer brand strength. The departing CPO should participate in shaping this narrative - Executive search: Initiate the search immediately upon notification — CPO searches take 4-6 months typically. Consider whether an internal candidate is ready or whether the board wants an external search - Create a "people strategy legacy document" that captures: the strategic architecture, key decisions and their rationale, what worked, what would be done differently, and recommended strategic priorities for the next 3-5 years - Establish advisory arrangement: 4-8 hours/month for 6-12 months minimum. CPO knowledge cannot be fully transferred in any notice period, and the successor will face situations where the departing CPO's counsel is invaluable - Protect the organization: Ensure all sensitive documents are properly catalogued and secured. The departing CPO has access to the company's most sensitive people information — handle this with appropriate care and legal counsel

Knowledge Transfer Checklist

Executive-specific items that apply across all levels (scope increases with level):

  • [ ] Strategic vision and rationale documented in comprehensive written form
  • [ ] Direct reports (M3s for E1, E1s for E2, E1s/E2s for E3) briefed individually with development context transferred to successor
  • [ ] All active management situations (FDPs, PIPs, performance reviews) for direct reports transferred
  • [ ] Executive and stakeholder relationships mapped and personally transitioned
  • [ ] Cross-functional partnerships (Finance, Legal, IT, business leadership) transitioned with context on joint initiatives
  • [ ] Board relationships and context transitioned (E2-E3)
  • [ ] Investor communication context and narrative framework transferred (E3)
  • [ ] All confidential matters (investigations, legal issues, executive compensation, restructuring plans) securely transferred to appropriate recipients
  • [ ] Budget, resource allocation, and vendor contract context documented
  • [ ] Succession plans and bench strength assessments documented at all levels within span
  • [ ] External relationships (industry, advisory boards, thought leadership commitments) mapped and transitioned
  • [ ] Organizational health assessment and risk register documented
  • [ ] Advisory arrangement terms established for post-departure support

Executive leadership team Day 1 CPO/CEO Departure, interim structure, strategic continuity

Level-Specific Communication Notes

E1 (Director): Communication extends across the HR function and to senior business stakeholders in the domain. The message focuses on domain strategy continuity — the direction set by the departing Director will continue, programs are on track, and the interim leadership is capable. Name the interim leader and their authority level explicitly.

E2 (Senior Director): Communication reaches the executive team, full HR function, and all senior business stakeholders. The message addresses enterprise HR continuity — the function's strategic direction is sound, cross-domain coordination will continue, and the CPO has a clear plan. The narrative should reinforce organizational stability and the strength of the HR leadership bench.

E3 (Chief People Officer): Communication is company-wide and potentially external. The CEO and board must be directly involved in crafting and delivering the message. Key audiences include: the board (confidence in succession), the executive team (strategic continuity), the HR function (stability and career continuity), all employees (the culture and people agenda continue), investors (organizational resilience), and industry (professional departure narrative). A poorly handled CPO departure can trigger retention concerns across the company; a well-handled one reinforces organizational maturity.

Exit Interview Focus Areas

Standard Questions (All Executive Levels)

  • What is your assessment of the HR function's strategic capability and readiness for the company's next phase?
  • How effective was the organization at supporting your leadership and enabling your impact as an executive?
  • What are the most important investments the HR function and the company should make in people strategy over the next 2-3 years?
  • How effective was cross-functional executive collaboration on people matters? Where did it break down?
  • What organizational or structural barriers limited the HR function's effectiveness or your ability to lead it?
  • Are there talent risks, succession gaps, or organizational health concerns that deserve immediate attention from your successor?
  • What would you tell your successor on their first day that no document can capture?

Level-Specific Questions

E1 (Director): - Did you have sufficient strategic authority over your domain to drive the outcomes you believed were necessary? - How effective was the executive team at integrating people strategy into business decisions? - What strategic risks exist within your domain that should be prioritized? - How could the organization better develop and retain HR executives?

E2 (Senior Director): - What is your assessment of the company's overall people strategy and its alignment with business direction? - How effective was the partnership with the CPO? What would make it more effective for your successor? - Are there enterprise-wide organizational changes or people investments the company is underinvesting in? - What is your candid assessment of the HR executive bench — who is ready for the next level, and where are the gaps?

E3 (Chief People Officer): - What is your assessment of the company's long-term talent competitiveness and organizational health? - Are there industry shifts, workforce trends, or competitive threats the organization is underestimating? - What is your candid assessment of the executive leadership team's overall strength and succession readiness? - How could the company better position people strategy as a genuine competitive advantage rather than a support function? - What commitments have you made externally (to candidates, partners, or the industry) that your successor should honor? - If you could redesign the CPO role based on what you learned, what would you change?

Manager Checklist — By Phase

Notification Phase (Day 0-1)

  • [ ] Acknowledge the departure; immediately notify the appropriate executive (CPO for E1-E2; CEO for E3)
  • [ ] For E3: CEO notifies the board within 24 hours
  • [ ] Identify and confirm interim leadership — who leads, with what authority, and for what duration
  • [ ] Assess all sensitive matters the departing executive is handling: legal issues, investigations, executive compensation, restructuring plans, board items
  • [ ] Secure access to critical documents and ensure no information gaps exist before the transition begins
  • [ ] Initiate executive search process if external search is likely (E2-E3 searches take months)
  • [ ] Draft communication plan for all audiences per the communication sequence above
  • [ ] For E3: engage communications team on external narrative

Transition Phase (Notice Period)

  • [ ] Create comprehensive knowledge transfer plan with structured sessions and milestones
  • [ ] Begin daily knowledge transfer sessions between departing executive and successor/interim
  • [ ] Conduct individual development and succession briefings for all direct reports (M3s/E1s/E2s)
  • [ ] Transfer all active management situations and sensitive matters with complete documentation
  • [ ] Execute communication plan per sequence — direct reports first, then outward
  • [ ] Transition executive relationships through joint meetings with successor
  • [ ] For E1: transition cross-functional partnerships and domain stakeholder relationships
  • [ ] For E2: transition C-suite partnerships, cross-domain coordination, and CPO partnership model
  • [ ] For E3: execute board transition (joint presentation with successor), CEO transition meetings, investor communication handoff
  • [ ] Transfer external relationships with personal introductions
  • [ ] Commission strategic documentation (domain assessment, state of function, legacy document)
  • [ ] Negotiate advisory arrangement terms
  • [ ] For E3: coordinate external announcement and narrative
  • [ ] Plan appropriate recognition (scale to the executive's tenure and impact)
  • [ ] Conduct exit interview (by CPO for E1-E2; by CEO and/or board chair for E3)

Final Day

  • [ ] Conduct final meeting — acknowledge contributions, confirm all transitions are complete
  • [ ] Verify all strategic documents are complete and delivered to appropriate recipients
  • [ ] Confirm all confidential materials are properly secured and transferred
  • [ ] Verify advisory arrangement terms are finalized and documented
  • [ ] Confirm IT/security access revocation — executive-level access to HRIS, employee data, compensation systems, board materials, and strategic documents
  • [ ] Collect company equipment and property
  • [ ] Ensure the HR function and broader organization know the plan going forward
  • [ ] For E3: ensure board has confidence in interim plan and search timeline

Post-Departure (Weeks 1-12)

  • [ ] Check in with direct reports on transition effectiveness and leadership support (weekly for first month)
  • [ ] Monitor organizational confidence and address concerns proactively
  • [ ] Utilize advisory arrangement for questions and issues that arise
  • [ ] For E2-E3: check in with executive team on cross-functional partnership continuity
  • [ ] For E3: provide board with transition progress updates
  • [ ] Monitor engagement and retention signals across the HR function and broader organization
  • [ ] Track executive search progress and adjust interim arrangements as needed
  • [ ] Address strategic knowledge gaps as they surface
  • [ ] At 30, 60, and 90 days: assess whether the transition plan is working and adjust
  • [ ] Review exit interview insights and incorporate into organizational improvement initiatives
