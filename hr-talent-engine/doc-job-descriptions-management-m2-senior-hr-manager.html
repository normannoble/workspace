Senior HR Manager

Level: M2 (Manager / Senior Manager)

Role Overview

The Senior HR Manager is a proficient people leader who manages with independence and confidence across multiple HR disciplines. This role may involve leading a larger HR team (8-15 people), managing M1 HR Managers, or owning significant HR programs that span Talent Acquisition, HR Operations, HRBP, L&D, and Compensation & Benefits. The emphasis is on operating effectively without significant oversight from senior leadership.

Success in this role requires handling complex people situations independently, building high-performing HR teams, and creating systems and practices that help the HR function scale with organizational growth. Senior HR Managers make sound people decisions confidently—hiring, performance management, promotions, and when necessary, terminations. They develop M1 managers into effective leaders, coordinate delivery across HR disciplines, and represent the HR function credibly in cross-functional forums.

While management is the primary focus, Senior HR Managers retain deep HR domain credibility and contribute to the design and delivery of major HR programs. They understand the interdependencies across HR disciplines well enough to optimize for overall function effectiveness rather than any single discipline's priorities.

Success Outcomes

Success in this role means achieving the following outcomes:

High-Performing Teams

  • HR teams that consistently deliver high-quality work across disciplines, meet commitments, and maintain sustainable pace through demanding HR cycles
  • Strong team culture with high engagement, psychological safety, and low regrettable attrition—particularly important in a function that regularly handles emotionally demanding work
  • Team members and M1 managers showing meaningful career growth, with clear development plans and active coaching
  • A reputation for HR team excellence within the organization—business leaders trust and want to partner with your teams

Independent People Management

  • Complex people situations handled effectively without significant manager oversight: performance issues, conflicts, retention risks, difficult conversations, and the unique challenges of managing HR professionals who themselves are people experts
  • Sound people decisions made confidently: hiring strong HR talent, compensation, promotions, and terminations within the HR function
  • Successful performance management including improvement plans when needed, with fair and consistent application
  • Hiring processes that consistently identify and attract strong HR candidates who succeed on the team and grow within the function

Program Ownership and Cross-Discipline Coordination

  • Significant HR programs or functional areas owned end-to-end, with clear accountability, consistent quality, and measurable outcomes
  • Effective coordination across HR disciplines—ensuring that recruiting, onboarding, HRBP support, L&D programming, and compensation practices work as an integrated system rather than disconnected silos
  • HR process improvements that measurably increase efficiency, employee experience, or compliance—implemented through the team rather than through personal heroics
  • Business cycle readiness—annual performance reviews, compensation planning, open enrollment, and hiring surges managed smoothly with appropriate resourcing and planning

Manager Development

  • M1 managers who are growing in capability as people leaders, receiving regular coaching and feedback on their management practice
  • Delegation that develops M1 managers while ensuring quality outcomes—not just task assignment, but genuine development of management judgment and confidence
  • A management culture within the HR teams that reflects shared practices, standards, and expectations
  • Early identification and development of future management candidates from the IC ranks

Strategic Alignment and Stakeholder Partnership

  • HR team strategy clearly aligned with organizational objectives and overall HR function priorities, with measurable progress toward goals
  • Strong partnerships with business leaders built on trust, transparency, and reliable delivery of HR services and programs
  • Cross-functional collaboration with Finance, Legal, IT, and other functions that improves HR program delivery and organizational outcomes
  • Effective representation of the HR function in leadership forums—articulating HR priorities, resource needs, and program impact with clarity and credibility

Required Skills

People Leadership

  • Advanced coaching skills—develops HR professionals and M1 managers to high performance through effective feedback, stretch assignments, and deliberate skill-building
  • Performance management expertise including difficult conversations, improvement plans, and terminations—modeled with the same rigor expected of the HR function more broadly
  • Conflict resolution and mediation—resolves interpersonal issues within and across HR teams and builds healthy team dynamics
  • Retention strategies—understands what motivates HR professionals at different career stages and creates conditions for engagement and growth

HR Domain Expertise

  • Broad HR knowledge (typically IC3-IC4 level depth in at least one discipline) with working fluency across Talent Acquisition, HR Operations, HRBP, L&D, and Compensation & Benefits
  • Strong understanding of employment law, regulatory compliance, and risk management across multiple HR domains
  • Program design and delivery expertise—can architect, implement, and measure the impact of HR programs at organizational scale
  • Data fluency—comfortable using HR analytics, workforce data, and operational metrics to inform decisions, build business cases, and demonstrate program impact

Communication and Collaboration

  • Stakeholder management across functions—builds trust with business leaders and navigates competing priorities between organizational needs and HR capacity
  • Executive communication—articulates HR team status, program impact, resource needs, and strategic priorities clearly to senior leaders
  • Change management—leads HR teams and the broader organization through process changes, system implementations, and policy updates while maintaining productivity and morale
  • Hiring excellence for the HR function—designs effective interview processes for HR roles and accurately assesses candidates across multiple disciplines

Core Competencies

  • Independent judgment—makes sound decisions about HR priorities, team management, and program trade-offs without requiring validation from senior leadership
  • Systems thinking—understands how HR disciplines interconnect and creates processes that optimize for overall function effectiveness rather than discipline-level efficiency
  • Cross-discipline fluency—can manage teams across multiple HR disciplines and understand enough about each to set appropriate expectations, evaluate quality, and identify risks
  • Resilience—maintains effectiveness and team health through the sustained pressure of HR work, including demanding business cycles and sensitive organizational situations

Required Experience

Years of Experience

  • 8-12 years of professional HR experience across multiple disciplines
  • 3-5 years in HR management, with demonstrated growth in capability and scope

Demonstrated Track Record

  • Built, inherited and significantly improved, or scaled HR teams across one or more disciplines
  • Successfully handled complex people situations within the HR function: performance management, conflict resolution, retention challenges, and managing through organizational change
  • Hired and developed multiple HR professionals who grew in their careers, including developing emerging managers
  • Delivered significant HR programs through teams with consistent quality and measurable business impact
  • Recognition as an effective HR manager from peers, direct reports, and leadership

Management Experience

  • Experience managing HR professionals across at least two disciplines, demonstrating the ability to lead beyond a single area of domain expertise
  • Track record of developing M1 managers or team leads into more effective people leaders
  • Experience managing through full HR business cycles—performance reviews, compensation planning, open enrollment, annual hiring plans—with teams that delivered consistently

HR Domain Experience

  • Deep hands-on experience in at least one HR discipline with working knowledge across the broader HR function
  • Track record of owning and delivering HR programs at organizational scale—not just contributing to them but being accountable for outcomes
  • Experience with HRIS systems, HR analytics, and the operational infrastructure that enables HR service delivery
  • Exposure to organizational design, change management, or other strategic HR work beyond day-to-day operations

Education

  • Bachelor's degree in Human Resources, Business Administration, Psychology, Organizational Development, or related field—or equivalent practical experience
  • HR certifications (SHRM-SCP, SPHR) valued but not required
