Level Context: What E1 Looks Like

E1 is an executive HR leader who owns a significant HR domain with full accountability for its success across the organization. Evaluate for:

  • Domain ownership — Full accountability for an HR domain's business impact, not just program execution
  • Executive leadership — Contributes to company strategy as an executive participant
  • Developing senior leaders — Grows M3s toward Director-level capability
  • Strategic people leadership — Connects people investments to business outcomes over 1-3 year horizons

Differentiation from M3: E1 operates as an executive with domain ownership and strategic accountability; M3 leads the HR function operationally. Differentiation from E2: E1 owns a specific HR domain; E2 leads across multiple domains with enterprise-wide perspective.

Competency Assessment

1. Domain Ownership & Strategic Accountability

What to assess: Ability to own an HR domain end-to-end with measurable business value and strategic vision.

E1 Expectations: - Delivers measurable business value from their HR domain with clear connection between people investments and company outcomes - Sets strategic vision for the domain that anticipates organizational needs 1-3 years ahead - Creates program quality that drives competitive advantage through people, not just operational compliance - Owns domain outcomes fully—program effectiveness, adoption, employee experience, and business impact—without requiring escalation

Sample Questions: - Tell me about an HR domain you owned. How did you connect its performance to business outcomes? - Walk me through your strategic vision for an HR domain. How did you anticipate needs 1-3 years ahead? - How do you measure whether your HR domain is creating competitive advantage, not just meeting operational standards? - Describe a time you took full ownership of a domain outcome—including when results fell short of expectations.

2. Executive Leadership & Cross-Functional Partnership

What to assess: Executive presence, contribution to company strategy, and ability to partner at the executive level.

E1 Expectations: - Contributes to company strategy as an executive participant, bringing people-informed perspective to business decisions - Builds cross-functional executive partnerships with Finance, Technology, Operations, and other functions - Makes strategic trade-offs with company-level perspective, balancing domain investments against organizational priorities - Communicates with leadership, board (when applicable), and external stakeholders with confidence and credibility

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a time you shaped a company-level decision by bringing the people perspective. What was the outcome? - Describe an executive partnership you built outside of HR. How did it create value for the company? - Walk me through a strategic trade-off you made—balancing your domain's priorities against broader organizational needs. - How do you communicate people strategy and program impact to executive leadership or board members?

3. Developing Senior Leaders

What to assess: Ability to develop M3s toward Director-level capability, build bench strength, and create a leadership culture.

E1 Expectations: - Develops M3s (Heads of HR) toward Director-level capability through deliberate development and increasing strategic exposure - Builds a leadership bench within the domain—succession planning ensures continuity and creates growth opportunities - Creates a management culture that exemplifies company values, models inclusive leadership, and develops talent at all levels - Ensures HR business partners and specialists are thriving with clear paths for growth

Sample Questions: - Tell me about an M3 or senior HR leader you developed. How did you increase their strategic capability? - How do you approach succession planning at the senior level? Give me a specific example. - Describe the leadership culture you've built within your domain. 4. Organizational Health & Scalability

What to assess: Building programs that scale, maintaining high engagement, and ensuring sustainable pace.

E1 Expectations: - Builds programs and practices that scale—growth in headcount increases capability without proportional complexity - Maintains high engagement and retention across the HR function, with the team known as a destination for strong talent - Fosters an inclusive, data-informed culture where diverse perspectives drive better people outcomes - Ensures sustainable pace—program delivery achieved without burning out the HR team

Sample Questions: - How have you built HR programs that scaled with organizational growth? What design choices mattered most? - Tell me about how you've maintained engagement and retention on your HR team, especially during demanding periods. - How do you use data to inform people decisions and demonstrate program impact within your domain? - Describe a time you identified that your team's pace was unsustainable. What did you do?

5. Judgment & Decision-Making

What to assess: Strategic judgment, accountability orientation, ability to make sound decisions under ambiguity at high stakes.

E1 Expectations: - Makes sound decisions with incomplete information at high stakes, particularly when balancing employee and business needs - Owns outcomes fully—doesn't deflect to circumstances or other functions - Builds alignment across executives with competing priorities on people matters - Maintains effectiveness through organizational challenges, difficult employee situations, and external pressures

Sample Questions: - Tell me about the highest-stakes people decision you've made. Walk me through your reasoning and the outcome. - Describe a time you had to build alignment among executives who disagreed on a people investment or strategy. - How do you handle situations where employee needs and business needs are genuinely in tension? - Tell me about a time an outcome in your domain fell short. How did you take accountability and course-correct?
