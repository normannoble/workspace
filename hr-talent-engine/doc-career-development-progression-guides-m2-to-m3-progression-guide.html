Progression Guide: M2 to M3

Senior HR Manager to Head of HR

Overview

The transition from M2 (Senior HR Manager) to M3 (Head of HR) represents the shift from effective independent management to senior leadership of a significant portion of or the entire HR function. This is the move from "mastering your craft" to "shaping how the function operates."

At M2, you manage teams independently, own significant programs, and coordinate across HR disciplines. At M3, you develop other managers into effective leaders, build organizational capability that scales, establish function-wide standards, and contribute directly to company strategy. You're accountable not just for team performance but for how HR as a function enables business performance.

This transition typically takes 3-5 years at M2 and requires demonstrating sustained senior leadership impact--building organizations, developing managers, and shaping strategy, not just managing well.

The Core Shift

M2 (Senior HR Manager) M3 (Head of HR)
Manages a team or multiple teams (8-15 people) Leads a significant portion of or the entire HR function (15+ people)
Operates independently without oversight Shapes how the HR function operates at organizational scale
Handles complex people situations confidently Develops M1 and M2 managers to handle complex situations
Owns significant HR programs end-to-end Establishes function-wide standards and practices across all disciplines
Cross-discipline coordination Cross-functional strategic partnership with Finance, Legal, IT, and business leadership
Manages M1 managers and develops emerging leaders Develops M1s and M2s into excellent people leaders with strategic capability
Represents HR in leadership forums Contributes directly to company strategy and executive discussions
Builds high-performing teams Builds a high-performing, scalable HR organization

The fundamental question changes from "Am I operating effectively without oversight?" to "Am I shaping how the HR function operates and developing the leaders who will sustain it?"**

Key Capabilities to Develop

1. Developing HR Managers into Leaders

At M2, you develop M1 managers and individual contributors. At M3, you develop M1s and M2s into excellent people leaders--not just effective HR practitioners who happen to manage, but leaders with strategic thinking, cross-discipline fluency, and sound organizational judgment.

What this looks like:

  • HR managers you develop are growing in leadership capability, not just HR execution
  • You delegate genuine organizational decisions to develop managers' judgment, not just tasks
  • Succession planning ensures the HR function isn't dependent on any single manager
  • The next generation of HR leaders is being deliberately developed under your guidance
  • A management culture exists within HR with shared practices and standards that reflect the same rigor the function asks of the rest of the organization

How to develop this:

  • Shift focus from coaching on specific situations to developing leadership frameworks and judgment
  • Give M2 managers ownership of cross-discipline problems--stretch their scope beyond their comfort zone
  • Create opportunities for managers to interact with senior business stakeholders and executive forums
  • Discuss their career trajectory explicitly--what does the path to Head of HR look like for them?
  • Build a deliberate development plan for each manager that goes beyond HR skills to leadership capability

2. Building Organizational Capability at Scale

At M2, you create systems and practices for your teams. At M3, you build organizational capability across the entire HR function--establishing standards, designing structure, and creating conditions for HR excellence that scales with company growth.

What this looks like:

  • Multiple HR teams deliver consistently against strategic objectives across all five disciplines
  • The HR function operates as an integrated system rather than a collection of separate teams
  • Standards and practices you establish measurably improve service quality, employee experience, compliance, or efficiency
  • The function scales effectively with company growth without proportional increases in headcount or coordination overhead
  • Cross-discipline integration creates compounding value--recruiting feeds strong onboarding, onboarding connects to L&D, HRBP insights inform compensation strategy

How to develop this:

  • Study organizational design--how do effective HR functions structure themselves at different company sizes?
  • Look for patterns across your teams--what improvements would benefit the whole function?
  • Invest in HR technology and data infrastructure that enables service delivery at scale
  • Build feedback loops between disciplines so improvements compound rather than stay siloed
  • Focus on building capability, not just capacity--the function should get better, not just bigger

3. Strategic Leadership and Executive Contribution

At M2, you align your team's work with organizational objectives. At M3, you contribute directly to company strategy, bringing the people perspective to executive discussions on growth, organizational design, and culture.

What this looks like:

  • HR strategy is clearly aligned with company objectives, with measurable progress and regular executive visibility
  • Cross-functional partnerships with Finance, Legal, IT, and business leadership position HR as a strategic partner, not just a service function
  • Forward-looking planning anticipates organizational needs 6-18 months ahead--workforce planning, capability gaps, compliance changes
  • You represent the people perspective meaningfully in executive discussions, not just reporting on HR metrics
  • Business leaders see you as someone who understands their challenges and makes sound trade-offs on people matters

How to develop this:

  • Invest deeply in understanding the business--financials, competitive dynamics, growth strategy, market challenges
  • Practice speaking in business terms--connect every HR initiative to organizational outcomes
  • Build relationships with executive peers beyond transactional HR interactions
  • Develop a point of view on organizational strategy, not just HR strategy
  • Seek exposure to board-level or investor-level conversations about people and organizational health

4. Culture and Compliance at Scale

At M2, you build strong team culture. At M3, you shape company-wide people culture through HR programs, policies, and practices, while ensuring the organization's compliance posture is robust across all HR domains.

What this looks like:

  • A company-wide people culture that reflects organizational values--shaped deliberately through programs and practices
  • Compliance posture across all HR domains is robust, current, and appropriate to the organization's size and industry
  • Employment practices are fair, consistent, and legally defensible, with sound judgment about when consistency matters and when flexibility serves the organization better
  • The HR function is trusted by employees at all levels as a credible, fair, and effective steward of the employment relationship
  • Organizational issues are identified and addressed proactively before they escalate, leveraging HR's unique position as an organizational sensor

How to develop this:

  • Develop a deliberate culture strategy--what do you want the company to be known for as an employer?
  • Build a strong partnership with Legal to ensure compliance is integrated into HR operations, not bolted on
  • Create mechanisms for identifying organizational health issues early--engagement data, exit interviews, manager feedback loops
  • Study how organizations at your stage manage culture through growth and change
  • Balance the tension between standardization and flexibility--know when each is appropriate

Common Pitfalls

Being a super-M2 instead of a senior leader

M3 isn't "Senior HR Manager with more scope." It's a qualitatively different role focused on building organizational capability, developing leaders, and shaping strategy. Manage through your managers, don't out-manage them.

Staying too close to the work

If you're still personally handling complex situations that your M2s should own, you're not operating at M3 level. Your job is to develop managers who can handle those situations, not to handle them yourself.

Neglecting the strategic dimension

M3 requires contributing to company strategy, not just executing HR strategy. If you're not building executive relationships and bringing people perspective to business discussions, you're missing a key dimension of the role.

Developing HR practitioners instead of leaders

At M3, your development focus shifts from building HR skills to building leadership capability. If your managers are becoming better HR practitioners but not better leaders, you're developing at the wrong level.

Underinvesting in organizational design

As scope grows, how the HR function is structured matters enormously. If you're not thinking deliberately about organizational design, team structure, and role clarity, coordination overhead will consume capacity that should go toward impact.

How to Demonstrate Readiness

Promotion to M3 requires sustained demonstration of senior leadership impact over 12-18+ months. Here's how to build your case:

Build a Track Record

  • Document HR managers you've developed and their growth in leadership capability
  • Track organizational outcomes across the HR function--not just individual team results but function-wide performance
  • Gather feedback from executive stakeholders about your strategic contribution and partnership effectiveness
  • Show evidence of function-wide standards, practices, or organizational improvements you've driven
  • Demonstrate forward-looking planning that anticipated and addressed organizational needs

Self-Assessment Questions

  1. Am I developing M1s and M2s into genuine leaders, not just effective HR practitioners?
  2. Does the HR function I lead operate as an integrated system, not a collection of separate teams?
  3. Am I contributing meaningfully to company strategy, not just executing HR strategy?
  4. Have I built organizational capability that scales--standards, practices, and structures that work at growth?
  5. Do executive stakeholders see me as a strategic partner, not just a functional leader?
  6. Is succession planning in place so the HR function doesn't depend on any single manager?
  7. Am I shaping company-wide people culture deliberately, not just managing HR team culture?

If you can answer "yes" to most of these consistently over 12-18+ months, you're likely ready for M3.

Typical Timeline

Most Senior HR Managers progress from M2 to M3 in 3-5 years. This timeframe allows for:

  • Developing multiple managers and seeing the results of that development over time
  • Building organizational capability that demonstrates sustainability, not just initial design
  • Establishing strategic credibility with executive leadership through consistent contribution
  • Creating a track record of function-wide impact, not just team-level success
  • Demonstrating the judgment and leadership presence required for a senior leadership role

Remember: M3 is about building and leading an HR organization, not just managing HR teams well. You need to demonstrate sustained senior leadership impact--developing leaders, shaping strategy, and building capability at scale. Not everyone needs to reach M3--many excellent Senior HR Managers have fulfilling careers leading HR programs and teams. Pursue M3 only if organizational leadership and executive contribution genuinely align with your interests and strengths.
