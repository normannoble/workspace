Level Context: What IC4 Looks Like

IC4 is a strategic learning leader who shapes enterprise-wide learning strategy, designs high-impact leadership development programs, and drives the decisions that determine how the organization builds capability at scale. Evaluate for:

  • Enterprise strategy ownership — Defines how the organization approaches learning and development, not just individual programs
  • Organizational capability building — Identifies capability gaps others haven't recognized and designs interventions at scale
  • Function leadership — Mentors senior L&D specialists, raises quality standards, and establishes best practices across the function
  • Business impact demonstration — Builds evaluation frameworks that credibly demonstrate ROI and sustain executive investment

Differentiation from IC3: IC3 owns significant program areas; IC4 shapes enterprise learning strategy and defines how the organization builds capability. Differentiation from IC5/Executive: IC4 operates as an expert individual contributor influencing strategy; executive roles carry organizational accountability for the L&D function itself.

Competency Assessment

1. Enterprise Learning Strategy

What to assess: Ability to define and drive enterprise learning strategy, proactively identify organizational capability gaps, make strategic technology decisions, and translate business objectives into learning priorities.

IC4 Expectations: - An enterprise learning strategy that is clearly articulated, aligned with business priorities, and guides investment decisions - Organizational capability gaps identified and addressed proactively based on strategic direction, workforce data, and industry trends - Learning technology decisions evaluated and recommended with clear rationale connecting technology to learning outcomes and scale - A strategic planning process that translates business objectives into capability development priorities

Sample Questions: - Walk me through an enterprise learning strategy you developed. How did you align it with business priorities and how did it guide investment decisions? - Tell me about a time you identified an organizational capability gap before leadership recognized it. How did you identify it and what did you do? - Describe a learning technology decision you led. How did you evaluate options and connect the choice to learning outcomes at scale? - How do you translate business objectives into L&D priorities? Give me a specific example of how you built the strategic planning process.

2. Leadership & Organizational Development

What to assess: Ability to design leadership development programs with measurable outcomes, build organizational capability frameworks, support change management with learning interventions, and inform succession planning.

IC4 Expectations: - Leadership development programs that demonstrably prepare leaders for increased scope—visible in promotion readiness, engagement scores, and bench strength - Organizational capability frameworks that define what "good" looks like across critical skill areas - Change management and organizational development initiatives supported with learning interventions that accelerate adoption - Succession planning and high-potential development informed by L&D insights

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a leadership development program you designed. How did you measure whether it actually prepared leaders for increased scope and complexity? - Describe an organizational capability framework you built. How did it create a shared language for development and assessment? - Give me an example of how you supported a major organizational change with learning interventions. What was the change and how did learning accelerate adoption? - How do you connect L&D strategy to succession planning and high-potential development? Give me a specific example.

3. L&D Function Excellence & Mentorship

What to assess: Ability to develop senior L&D professionals, raise quality standards across the function, establish best practices that are adopted at scale, and build learning communities of practice.

IC4 Expectations: - Senior L&D Specialists (IC3s) growing toward Principal level because of effective mentorship and deliberate development investment - Quality standards for program design, delivery, and evaluation raised across the function through influence and exemplary work - Best practices, frameworks, and methodologies established and adopted across the L&D team - A learning community of practice extending beyond the L&D team, positioning L&D as a center of expertise

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a senior L&D professional you developed. What was your approach and how did their capabilities grow? - Describe how you raised quality standards across an L&D function. What was the standard before, what did you change, and how did you drive adoption? - Give me an example of a framework or methodology you established that was adopted across an L&D team. How did you get buy-in? - How have you built a community of practice around learning? What did it look like and what was the impact?

4. Measurement, Impact & Thought Leadership

What to assess: Ability to demonstrate business impact credibly, conduct portfolio-level analysis, synthesize data into strategic recommendations, and provide thought leadership.

IC4 Expectations: - Evaluation frameworks that credibly demonstrate business impact (Kirkpatrick Level 4 and ROI) for major learning investments - Portfolio-level analysis of learning programs that identifies what to invest in, redesign, or retire - Insights synthesized from learning analytics, industry research, and organizational data into strategic recommendations - Thought leadership within the organization and externally that establishes the company's L&D reputation

Sample Questions: - Walk me through an evaluation framework you built that demonstrated business impact (Level 4 or ROI) for a major learning investment. How did you set it up and what did you learn? - Describe a time you conducted a portfolio-level analysis of learning programs. What did you find and what decisions resulted? - How do you synthesize data from multiple sources (learning analytics, research, organizational data) into strategic recommendations? Give me an example. - Tell me about your thought leadership activities. How have you contributed to the L&D field internally or externally?

5. Organizational Influence & Systems Thinking

What to assess: Ability to influence without authority, communicate at the executive level, build cross-functional coalitions, and think in systems about how learning intersects with talent management and business strategy.

IC4 Expectations: - Influence without authority—builds consensus around learning strategy and drives change across business units and executive leadership - Executive communication—frames learning strategy, investment rationale, and impact data for C-suite audiences - Coalition building—aligns diverse stakeholders (HR, business leaders, IT, finance) around learning priorities - Systems thinking—understands how learning strategy intersects with talent management, organizational design, performance management, and business strategy

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a time you had to influence executive leadership to invest in a learning initiative they were skeptical about. How did you make the case? - Describe how you communicate L&D strategy and impact to C-suite or senior leadership audiences. Give me a specific example. - Walk me through a cross-functional coalition you built to advance a learning initiative. Who were the stakeholders and how did you align them? - How do you think about the intersection of learning strategy with talent management, organizational design, and business strategy? Give me an example where systems thinking changed your approach.
