Junior HR Business Partner

Level: IC2 (Contributing)

Role Overview

The Junior HR Business Partner supports the delivery of people strategies within assigned business groups, working alongside a senior HRBP to handle operational business partnership tasks. This role focuses on building foundational HRBP capabilities—learning to translate business context into people solutions, managing routine employee relations cases, and ensuring reliable execution of HR programs within their scope.

Success in this role requires developing strong relationships with frontline managers, building credibility through consistent follow-through, and growing toward independent business partnership. Junior HR Business Partners are expected to own operational HRBP workstreams with increasing autonomy while knowing when to escalate complex situations to their senior partner. This is a role where pattern recognition and professional judgment are actively developing—each employee relations case, talent review cycle, and manager coaching conversation builds the foundation for strategic partnership at higher levels.

Success Outcomes

Success in this role means achieving the following outcomes:

Strategic Business Partnership

  • Frontline managers within assigned groups who view the Junior HRBP as a reliable first point of contact for day-to-day people questions—building the trust foundation that enables deeper partnership over time
  • Workforce data gathered and organized for senior HRBP review—including headcount trends, attrition patterns, and hiring pipeline status—that contributes to informed workforce planning decisions
  • Business context within assigned groups that is well-understood and documented, enabling the Junior HRBP to connect HR recommendations to the specific operational realities of their client teams
  • Organizational change communications and logistics supported effectively, ensuring that restructurings, team moves, and leadership transitions are executed smoothly within assigned groups

Talent Management & Development

  • Talent review logistics and data preparation within assigned groups executed accurately and on time—enabling senior HRBPs and business leaders to focus calibration time on discussion rather than data gathering
  • Manager coaching on routine talent development topics (onboarding new hires, setting development goals, having career conversations) that helps frontline managers build basic people management habits
  • Performance concerns flagged early through regular manager check-ins and engagement data monitoring—surfacing issues to the senior HRBP before they become formal performance management situations
  • Internal mobility opportunities and learning resources surfaced proactively to managers and employees within assigned groups, contributing to development and retention

Employee Relations & Organizational Health

  • Routine employee relations cases (interpersonal conflicts, policy questions, accommodation requests, low-complexity performance situations) handled independently with appropriate outcomes, proper documentation, and timely resolution
  • Complex or sensitive employee relations situations recognized and escalated to the senior HRBP with clear context, relevant facts gathered, and a preliminary assessment—demonstrating growing judgment about when independent action is appropriate
  • Engagement survey data, exit interview themes, and pulse check insights synthesized into actionable summaries for assigned groups, contributing to the organizational health picture maintained by the senior HRBP
  • HR policies applied consistently within assigned groups, with questions about edge cases or context-specific exceptions escalated with a recommended approach rather than just the question

HR Program Execution & Feedback

  • Enterprise HR programs (performance review cycles, compensation planning, engagement surveys, benefits enrollment) deployed within assigned groups with high participation rates, clean data, and timely completion
  • Manager and employee questions about HR programs answered accurately and promptly, reducing the volume of escalations to HR Centers of Excellence and freeing the senior HRBP to focus on strategic work
  • Feedback from managers and employees about HR program effectiveness collected systematically and channeled to the senior HRBP and HR Centers of Excellence—ensuring that on-the-ground realities inform program design
  • Process improvements to HR program execution identified from hands-on experience and implemented or proposed—demonstrating initiative in making recurring processes more efficient and effective

Required Skills

HR & Business Expertise

  • Foundational knowledge across core HR disciplines including employee relations, talent management, compensation basics, and employment law fundamentals
  • Developing business acumen—ability to understand the goals, challenges, and operational rhythms of assigned business groups well enough to contextualize HR support
  • Working knowledge of HR systems and tools for data management, reporting, and program administration
  • Familiarity with basic organizational assessment approaches—can interpret engagement data, attrition metrics, and workforce demographics with guidance

Leadership & Collaboration

  • Relationship-building skills with frontline managers—establishing credibility through responsiveness, follow-through, and practical guidance
  • Effective partnership with senior HRBP—proactive communication about workload, escalations, and emerging issues without requiring constant check-ins
  • Collaboration with HR Centers of Excellence (recruiting, compensation, L&D, HR operations) to coordinate program delivery and resolve cross-functional questions

Core Competencies

  • Developing professional judgment in sensitive situations—can assess the severity and complexity of employee relations issues and determine appropriate next steps or escalation points
  • Strong organizational skills and attention to detail—able to manage multiple concurrent HR processes across assigned groups without dropping threads
  • Written and verbal communication skills appropriate for HR contexts—can document employee relations cases clearly, draft manager communications, and present data summaries
  • Discretion and integrity in handling confidential information—understands the trust required in an HR role and consistently maintains appropriate boundaries

Required Experience

Years of Experience

  • 2-5 years of progressive HR experience, including some exposure to generalist or HRBP work (not purely specialist or administrative)

Demonstrated Track Record

  • Experience handling routine employee relations cases with appropriate outcomes and documentation
  • History of supporting HR program execution (performance cycles, compensation planning, engagement surveys) with attention to detail and timely completion
  • Evidence of building productive working relationships with managers and employees in a support or advisory capacity
  • Demonstrated growth in professional judgment and increasing independence over the course of their HR career

Domain Experience

  • Hands-on experience with HRIS platforms and HR reporting tools
  • Exposure to talent review or calibration processes, even in a supporting or data-preparation capacity
  • Experience working in an HR team environment with multiple disciplines (recruiting, compensation, L&D) and understanding of how HRBP work connects to these functions

Education

  • Bachelor's degree in Human Resources, Business Administration, Psychology, or related field—or equivalent practical experience
  • HR certifications (SHRM-CP, PHR) are valued but not required
