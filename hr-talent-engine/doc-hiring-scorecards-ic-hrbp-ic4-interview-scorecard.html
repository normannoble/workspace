Level Context: What IC4 Looks Like

IC4 is a strategic people leader who partners with VP and SVP-level executives on the most complex organizational challenges—large-scale org design, M&A people integration, culture transformation, and enterprise talent strategy. Evaluate for:

  • Executive advisory — VP/SVP-level leaders treat the Senior HRBP as an indispensable strategic advisor on organizational decisions
  • Enterprise-scale impact — Drives initiatives across major business segments with measurable business outcomes
  • Complex judgment — Handles the most sensitive organizational situations (executive misconduct, systemic cultural issues, high-profile departures) with strategic awareness
  • HRBP development — Mentors other HRBPs and defines the standard for business partnership quality

Differentiation from IC3: IC3 owns people strategy for business units; IC4 partners with VP/SVP executives on enterprise-scale challenges and mentors other HRBPs. Differentiation from IC5/Executive: IC4 operates as an expert individual contributor; executive roles carry organizational accountability for the HRBP function itself.

Competency Assessment

1. Strategic Business Partnership

What to assess: Ability to serve as a strategic advisor to VP/SVP-level executives, drive enterprise-level people strategies, lead large-scale org design, and conduct strategic workforce planning.

IC4 Expectations: - VP/SVP-level leaders treat the Senior HRBP as an indispensable strategic advisor, actively involving them in business planning and strategic pivots - People strategies for major business segments that demonstrably contribute to business outcomes with measurable improvements - Organizational design initiatives at scale (restructurings, M&A integration, new business unit creation) executed with minimal disruption and lasting outcomes - Workforce planning at the strategic level—anticipating capability gaps and leadership pipeline risks 12-24 months out

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a time you were involved in a senior leader's business planning process—not just the people portion, but the broader strategic discussion. What was your contribution? - Describe a large-scale organizational design initiative you led. What was the scope, what was your approach, and how did the outcomes hold up over time? - Walk me through a people strategy you developed for a major business segment. How did you connect it to measurable business outcomes? - How do you approach strategic workforce planning at the 12-24 month horizon? Give me a specific example where your forward-looking analysis changed a leadership team's investment decisions.

2. Talent Management & Development

What to assess: Ability to drive enterprise talent strategy, coach executives, design cross-functional mobility programs, and run calibration across large organizations.

IC4 Expectations: - Talent strategy across supported business segments that identifies and accelerates development of the organization's most critical leaders - Executive coaching relationships that measurably improve senior leaders' effectiveness as people leaders - Talent mobility and development programs that break down silos and retain high-potential talent - Calibration processes across large organizations that produce fair, defensible outcomes and build manager capability

3. Employee Relations & Organizational Health

What to assess: Ability to handle the most complex ER situations, monitor organizational health at a systemic level, lead culture transformation, and set consistent policy standards across diverse organizations.

IC4 Expectations: - The most complex and sensitive ER situations (executive misconduct, systemic cultural issues, high-profile departures, workplace investigations with legal exposure) handled with judgment, discretion, and strategic awareness - Organizational health monitored at a systemic level—identifying patterns across teams, leaders, and business cycles that reveal root causes - Culture transformation initiatives during significant organizational change that deliberately shape the target culture with measurable indicators - A consistent standard for HR policies across large, diverse organizations with judgment about when flexibility serves the organization better than consistency

Sample Questions: - Walk me through the most sensitive employee relations situation you've ever handled—one with executive involvement or significant legal or reputational exposure. How did you navigate it? - Describe a time you identified a systemic organizational health issue—not just a single team problem, but a pattern across multiple teams or leaders. What did you find and what did you do about it? - Tell me about a culture transformation initiative you led during a period of significant organizational change. How did you define the target culture and measure progress? - How do you determine when to enforce consistency in HR policy application versus when to allow context-appropriate flexibility? Give me a specific example.

4. HR Program Execution & HRBP Development

What to assess: Ability to influence enterprise HR strategy through business insight, champion large-scale HR transformations, establish systematic feedback loops, and develop other HRBPs.

IC4 Expectations: - Enterprise HR strategy influenced through deep business insight—ensuring company-wide programs reflect actual needs, not just HR best practices in the abstract - Large-scale HR transformations championed within supported business segments with adoption that reflects genuine behavior change - Systematic feedback loop between business reality and HR Centers of Excellence that shapes program design and investment priorities - Other HRBPs (IC2s and IC3s) developing faster because of the Senior HRBP's mentorship and role modeling

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a time you influenced enterprise HR strategy based on what you observed in the business. How did you make your case and what changed? - Describe a large-scale HR transformation you championed in your client group. How did you drive adoption beyond compliance—toward genuine behavior change? - How do you systematically channel business insights back to HR Centers of Excellence? Give me an example where your feedback meaningfully changed a program's design. - Tell me about an HRBP you mentored whose development you accelerated. What was your approach and how do you know it worked?

5. Executive Judgment & Organizational Navigation

What to assess: Judgment under ambiguity, systems thinking at the organizational level, strategic pattern recognition, and resilience in high-stakes situations.

IC4 Expectations: - Exceptional judgment under ambiguity and pressure—makes consequential decisions when information is incomplete, stakes are high, and multiple legitimate interests are in tension - Systems thinking at the organizational level—understands how decisions about structure, compensation, leadership, and culture interact across a large organization - Strategic pattern recognition—synthesizes signals from engagement data, attrition trends, leadership behavior, and market dynamics into a coherent organizational narrative - Resilience and composure in high-stakes situations—manages executive-level conflicts and organizational crises while maintaining objectivity

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a time you had to make a consequential organizational decision with incomplete information and competing legitimate interests. How did you navigate it? - Describe a situation where you connected patterns across multiple data sources—engagement surveys, attrition, leadership behavior, business performance—to identify something others hadn't seen. What did you find? - Give me an example of a time you had to manage an executive-level conflict or organizational crisis. How did you maintain objectivity and credibility with all parties? - How do you think about the long-term, cascading effects of organizational decisions? Give me an example where you helped a senior leader see second and third-order consequences they hadn't considered.
