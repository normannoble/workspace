Progression Guide: E1 to E2

Director of HR to Senior Director of HR

Overview

The transition from E1 (Director of HR) to E2 (Senior Director of HR) represents the shift from domain leadership to general management across the entire HR function. While Directors own specific HR domains (such as Talent & Development, Employee Experience & Operations, or Total Rewards & People Analytics) with full accountability, Senior Directors take a cross-cutting perspective--ensuring all of HR works together effectively to deliver a coherent employee experience.

At E1, you own a domain and operate as an executive participant. At E2, you're a strategic partner to the Chief People Officer, often deputizing in their absence. You develop Directors into future executive leaders, shape how HR leadership operates, drive organizational transformation through people, and represent the company's people capabilities externally.

This transition is primarily qualitative, not quantitative. It's not about managing more--it's about a fundamentally different type of work: from domain excellence to enterprise-wide HR leadership, from executive participation to executive partnership.

This transition typically takes 3-5+ years at E1, and E2 positions are rare. Many excellent Directors have fulfilling careers at E1--that's a significant executive achievement.

The Core Shift

E1 (Director of HR) E2 (Senior Director of HR)
Owns a specific HR domain General management across all HR domains
Executive participant Executive partner (to CPO)
Develops M3s toward Director Develops E1s toward Senior Director/CPO
Domain strategy (1-3 years) Enterprise people strategy (2-5 years)
Internal executive presence Internal + external executive presence
Accountable for domain outcomes Accountable for HR-function-wide outcomes

Board exposure when applicable Regular board presence expected

The fundamental question changes from "Am I accountable for my HR domain's success?" to "Am I shaping how the entire HR function succeeds as a strategic driver for the company?"**

Key Capabilities to Develop

1. Cross-Domain HR Leadership

At E1, you optimize your domain. At E2, you optimize across domains--ensuring all of HR works together to deliver a coherent employee experience and drive company outcomes, not just domain outcomes.

What this looks like:

  • Multiple HR domains under your influence work together seamlessly, delivering an integrated employee experience across the full talent lifecycle
  • You resolve cross-domain conflicts with lasting solutions, not temporary compromises
  • Resource allocation decisions optimize for company outcomes, not any single domain's priorities
  • Strategic coherence exists across all people programs--investments in hiring, development, compensation, and culture tell a consistent story
  • Problems that span HR domains get resolved through your leadership without requiring ongoing senior intervention

How to develop this:

  • Seek opportunities to lead cross-domain initiatives or resolve cross-domain tensions
  • Build deep relationships with peer Directors--understand their domains, challenges, and how their work connects to yours
  • Practice making recommendations that optimize for the whole function, even at your own domain's expense
  • Study organizational design--how do effective HR functions integrate their domains at scale?
  • Map the employee lifecycle end-to-end and identify where domain handoffs create friction or value gaps

2. Executive Partnership with the CPO

At E1, you participate in executive discussions. At E2, you're a true partner to the CPO--contributing to enterprise people strategy, not just executing on it.

What this looks like:

  • The CPO relies on you as a thought partner on major people decisions and organizational strategy
  • You can represent the CPO credibly in executive forums, board settings, or externally
  • You contribute to shaping enterprise people strategy, not just implementing it within your domain
  • Other executives see you as a peer who understands the whole HR function and its connection to business performance
  • In the CPO's absence, you're the natural leader of the people function

How to develop this:

  • Build a close working relationship with the CPO--understand their priorities, concerns, and how they think about enterprise people strategy
  • Volunteer to represent the CPO in forums where they can't attend
  • Contribute ideas and perspectives on company-level people strategy proactively, not just when asked
  • Develop relationships with board members or investors when opportunities arise
  • Practice thinking at the CPO level--what would you do if you had their scope and accountability?

3. Developing Executive Leaders

At E1, you develop M3s toward Director. At E2, you develop Directors (E1s) into future Senior Directors and CPO candidates.

What this looks like:

  • Directors you develop are growing toward Senior Director or CPO-level capability
  • You delegate significant organizational decisions to develop their executive judgment, not just their domain expertise
  • You create opportunities for Directors to operate at E2 level before they have the title
  • Succession planning means HR leadership isn't dependent on any individual
  • The executive leadership culture within HR models excellence for the broader organization

How to develop this:

  • Shift focus from developing domain capability to developing executive judgment and enterprise thinking
  • Give Directors ownership of cross-domain problems--stretch their scope beyond their domain
  • Create opportunities for Directors to interact with the board, investors, and external audiences
  • Discuss their path to Senior Director or CPO explicitly
  • Coach on organizational leadership and enterprise strategy, not just domain leadership

4. External Presence and Thought Leadership

At E1, your presence is primarily internal. At E2, you represent the company's people capabilities externally--building employer brand, attracting talent, and creating strategic value through industry relationships.

What this looks like:

  • You're recognized externally as a people and HR leader, not just internally
  • Your external presence attracts HR talent and creates partnership opportunities
  • You can represent the company credibly to candidates, customers, partners, investors, and industry peers
  • Thought leadership builds the company's reputation as a destination for exceptional talent
  • Industry relationships create strategic value--benchmarking insights, talent market intelligence, and partnerships

How to develop this:

  • Build external presence through speaking, writing, or HR community involvement
  • Represent the company at industry events, HR leadership forums, or candidate-facing settings
  • Develop relationships with people leaders at peer companies
  • Contribute to industry discourse on talent, culture, and the future of work
  • Make your external presence a deliberate part of your development plan, not an afterthought

Common Pitfalls

Optimizing for your domain instead of the organization

The biggest shift from E1 to E2 is from domain focus to enterprise-wide focus. If you're still primarily advocating for your domain's resources, priorities, and outcomes, you're not operating at E2.

Being a super-Director instead of an organizational leader

E2 isn't "Director with more domains." It's a qualitatively different role focused on how the entire HR function succeeds as a strategic driver for the business. Manage through your Directors, don't out-Direct them.

Neglecting external presence

E2s represent the company externally. If you're entirely internally focused, you're missing a key dimension of the role. The company's employer brand and reputation benefit from senior HR leaders who are visible in the industry.

Not building a true CPO partnership

E2 requires being a genuine thought partner to the CPO, not just an excellent executor. If you're not contributing to enterprise people strategy at the company level, you're not operating at E2.

Developing M3s instead of E1s

Your development focus shifts at E2. If you're still primarily developing M3s, you're not operating at the right level. Your Directors should be developing M3s--your focus is on growing Directors into senior executives.

How to Demonstrate Readiness

Promotion to E2 requires sustained demonstration of enterprise-level impact over 18-24+ months. Here's how to build your case:

Build a Track Record

  • Document cross-domain impact and organizational-level contributions beyond your domain
  • Track Directors you've developed and their trajectory toward senior executive roles
  • Gather feedback from the CPO about your partnership effectiveness and readiness for broader scope
  • Build external presence that creates tangible value for the company
  • Show evidence of enterprise people strategy contributions, not just domain strategy execution

Self-Assessment Questions

  1. Am I optimizing for organizational outcomes, not domain outcomes?
  2. Am I a true thought partner to the CPO on enterprise people strategy?
  3. Am I developing Directors toward Senior Director or CPO-level capability?
  4. Do I represent the company's people capabilities credibly externally?
  5. Can I deputize for the CPO effectively in their absence?
  6. Do cross-domain problems get resolved through my leadership with lasting solutions?
  7. Do other executives see me as someone who understands the entire people function, not just my domain?

If you can answer "yes" to most of these consistently over 18-24+ months, you may be ready for E2.

Typical Timeline

Most leaders who progress from E1 to E2 do so in 3-5+ years at E1. This reflects:

  • Building enterprise-level impact beyond domain success
  • Developing a true CPO partnership through sustained contribution
  • Growing Directors who demonstrate your executive development capability
  • Building meaningful external presence and industry relationships
  • E2 positions being very limited--often only 1-2 in an organization this size

Remember: E1 represents significant executive success. Many excellent Directors have fulfilling careers at E1 without reaching E2. Pursue E2 only if enterprise-wide HR leadership--CPO partnership, cross-domain accountability, external presence, and organizational transformation--genuinely aligns with your interests and strengths.
