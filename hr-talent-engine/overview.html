The Problem

Talent documents drift the moment they're written.

The job description you hire against says one thing. The performance review measures another. The career ladder points somewhere else entirely. You built the system once — and it's been drifting apart ever since.

The build

6–12 months of leadership and HR time to draft a full framework from scratch — RDs, ladders, review templates, hiring rubrics.

The drift

Documents are written in isolation and edited in isolation. Within a quarter of shipping, your RDs, reviews, and ladders are already telling different stories.

The cost

Unclear promotions. Reviews people don't trust. Hiring for roles that don't match the org anymore. Every leader building private workarounds.

The Diagnosis

You don't have a documentation problem. You have an architecture problem.

Most talent stacks are written as separate artifacts — each document a self-contained draft. There's no shared source, no cascade, no way to keep them in sync. Every edit is a fresh chance to drift.

Artifact approach

Documents drafted independently

  • RDs live in Google Docs
  • Review templates live in HRIS
  • Career ladders live on Notion
  • Hiring scorecards live in the ATS
  • Every edit happens in isolation
  • Reconciliation is a whole project

System approach

Documents derived from a single source

  • One outcome-based RD per role
  • Reviews derived from role outcomes
  • Ladders map level transitions
  • Scorecards test the same outcomes
  • Change the RD → everything can regenerate
  • Consistency is structural, not manual
The Insight

Make the Role Description the single source of truth.

An outcome-based RD isn't a task list. It's a definition of what success looks like at a level. Once success is defined in one place, every other document — from a hiring scorecard to a promotion decision — can be derived from it and stay aligned to it.

Outcome-based

Roles defined by what success looks like at each level, not what tasks are performed. Task lists rot; outcomes stay meaningful.

Level-calibrated

Every role explicit about how it differs from the level below and above. No ambiguity about what a promotion actually means.

Cascade-ready

Written in a structure downstream documents can consume. One canonical statement drives seven aligned artifacts.

The Mechanism

One outcome-based RD becomes nine aligned documents.

The Role Description sits at the root. Every downstream artifact — hiring, onboarding, performance, career, offboarding — inherits its language, its success criteria, its level bar. Change the RD, and the cascade can regenerate.

The Consistency Guarantee

Change one RD. Every downstream document regenerates to match.

This is the difference between owning a static document library and owning a living talent system. When your Senior Recruiter role evolves — new market realities, new team structure, new outcomes — the RD changes once. Everything downstream can be regenerated in a pass and stay aligned.

Without Talent Engine

Update the RD. Then remember to update the interview scorecard. And the onboarding plan. And the three assessment templates. And the progression guide. And the offboarding checklist. Miss one, and drift is back.

With Talent Engine

Update the RD. Regenerate the cascade. Every downstream document reflects the change — with the same language, the same success criteria, the same level bar. Consistency is a property of the system, not a discipline you have to enforce.
