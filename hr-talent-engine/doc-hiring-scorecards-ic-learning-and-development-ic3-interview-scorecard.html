Level Context: What IC3 Looks Like

IC3 is a self-directed learning professional who designs and delivers high-impact development programs that measurably improve employee capability, performance, and career growth. Evaluate for:

  • Needs analysis depth — Diagnoses performance gaps and recommends the right intervention, which isn't always training
  • Business impact orientation — Creates learning experiences that solve real organizational problems with measurable outcomes
  • Stakeholder partnership — Trusted advisor to business leaders on capability development, not just a training catalog
  • Learning ecosystem thinking — Builds a comprehensive learning environment including formal programs, peer learning, and knowledge management

Differentiation from IC2: IC3 conducts strategic needs analyses and measures business impact; IC2 designs and delivers assigned programs. Differentiation from IC4: IC3 owns significant program areas and influences strategy; IC4 shapes enterprise-wide learning strategy and drives the organization's approach to capability building.

Competency Assessment

1. Program Design & Delivery

What to assess: Ability to ground programs in business needs, apply adult learning principles effectively, deliver across multiple modalities, and maintain program currency.

IC3 Expectations: - Learning programs grounded in identified business needs and performance gaps, validated through needs analysis - Training content that applies adult learning principles effectively—engaging, relevant, paced for retention and behavior change - Programs delivered across multiple modalities (instructor-led, virtual, self-paced, blended) meeting learners where they are - Program content maintained through regular review cycles rather than becoming stale

Sample Questions: - Walk me through a program you designed that started with a needs analysis. How did you validate the actual need versus the stated request? - Describe your most effective blended learning program. How did you decide on the modality mix? - Tell me about a program you designed that demonstrably changed participant behavior, not just satisfaction scores. How do you know it worked? - How do you approach maintaining and refreshing programs over time? Give me an example of a program you significantly revised based on evolving needs.

2. Learning Impact & Measurement

What to assess: Ability to build evaluation frameworks beyond satisfaction scores, demonstrate program impact on performance gaps, communicate ROI, and use data for continuous improvement.

IC3 Expectations: - Evaluation frameworks that go beyond satisfaction (Level 1) to measure knowledge acquisition (Level 2), behavior change (Level 3), and business impact (Level 4) - Programs with demonstrated impact on the performance gaps they were designed to address - Stakeholder reports that communicate outcomes and ROI in terms business leaders understand - Insights from program data that inform continuous improvement decisions

Sample Questions: - How do you measure the impact of a learning program beyond participant satisfaction? Walk me through your evaluation framework for a specific program. - Tell me about a program where you were able to demonstrate business impact (Kirkpatrick Level 3 or 4). How did you set up the measurement and what did you find? - Describe how you communicate program ROI to business leaders. Give me an example of a presentation or report you created. - Tell me about a time evaluation data led you to retire, redesign, or significantly change a program. What was the data and what did you decide?

3. Stakeholder Partnership & Needs Analysis

What to assess: Ability to serve as a strategic partner for performance challenges, conduct performance consulting that diagnoses root causes, design with stakeholder input, and manage vendor relationships.

IC3 Expectations: - Business leaders and HRBPs who view L&D as a strategic partner for solving performance challenges - Performance consulting conversations that diagnose root causes before prescribing solutions—distinguishing skill gaps from process issues and motivation problems - Program design that incorporates stakeholder input, SME expertise, and learner perspective - Strong partnerships with external vendors and content providers that extend L&D capability

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a time a stakeholder came to you with a training request that turned out not to be a training problem. How did you diagnose the root cause and what did you recommend instead? - Describe how you conduct a needs analysis for a new program. Walk me through the specific steps and conversations. - How do you build trust with business leaders so they see L&D as a strategic partner rather than a training order-taker? - Tell me about a vendor or external facilitator relationship you managed. How did you ensure quality while extending your team's capacity?

4. Knowledge Management & Learning Culture

What to assess: Ability to build a learning ecosystem beyond courses, create valued flagship programs, foster knowledge-sharing practices, and contribute to a culture of continuous learning.

IC3 Expectations: - A learning ecosystem that includes formal programs, peer learning, knowledge repositories, and on-the-job resources - Onboarding, manager development, and leadership programs consistently cited as valuable by participants and managers - Knowledge-sharing practices and communities of practice that reduce tribal knowledge dependencies - Visible contribution to a culture of continuous learning where development is ongoing, not annual

Sample Questions: - Describe a learning ecosystem you helped build. How did you go beyond formal courses to create ongoing development opportunities? - Tell me about a flagship program (onboarding, manager development, or leadership) you created or significantly improved. What made it effective? - How have you fostered knowledge-sharing practices or communities of practice in your organization? - What does a "learning culture" mean to you? Give me an example of something specific you did to build one.

5. L&D Expertise & Mentorship

What to assess: Depth of instructional design and facilitation expertise, mentorship of junior team members, analytical skills, and continuous learning orientation.

IC3 Expectations: - Deep proficiency in instructional design methodologies (ADDIE, SAM, or equivalent) with strong command of adult learning theory - Stakeholder management ability—builds trust, conducts effective needs analyses, and pushes back constructively when training is not the right solution - Mentorship capability—develops junior L&D team members' skills in design, facilitation, and consulting - Continuous learning orientation—stays current with learning science, technology, and industry trends

Sample Questions: - Tell me about your instructional design philosophy. Which methodologies do you use and how have they evolved over your career? - Describe a time you pushed back on a stakeholder's request because training wasn't the right solution. How did you handle it? - Tell me about someone you've mentored in L&D. How did you help them grow their design, facilitation, or consulting skills? - What learning science, technology trends, or industry practices have you been exploring recently? How have they influenced your work?
