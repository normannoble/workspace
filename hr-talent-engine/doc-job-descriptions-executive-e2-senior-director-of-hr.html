Senior Director of HR

Level: E2 (Senior Director)

Role Overview

The Senior Director of HR is a senior executive who operates across multiple HR domains or owns the day-to-day leadership of the entire HR function. Where Directors own specific domains like Talent & Development or Total Rewards, the Senior Director takes a general management perspective—ensuring all of HR works together effectively, aligns with company strategy, and delivers a coherent employee experience.

This role serves as a close partner to the Chief People Officer, often deputizing in their absence and contributing directly to enterprise people strategy. Senior Directors develop Directors (E1s) into future executive leaders, shape how HR leadership operates, drive organizational transformation through people, and represent the company's people capabilities externally.

The shift from Director to Senior Director is primarily qualitative: from domain excellence to enterprise-wide HR leadership, from executive participation to executive partnership, and from developing senior managers to developing fellow executives. Senior Directors own the challenge of making HR a genuine competitive advantage—building the systems, culture, and leadership that enable the company to win through its people.

Success Outcomes

Success in this role means achieving the following outcomes:

Cross-Domain HR Leadership

  • Multiple HR domains that work together effectively, delivering a coherent and seamless employee experience across the full talent lifecycle
  • Organizational effectiveness across the HR function—coordination, prioritization, and resource allocation that optimizes for company outcomes, not domain outcomes
  • Strategic coherence across all people programs—investments in hiring, development, compensation, and culture tell a consistent story and build toward shared objectives
  • Problems that span HR domains resolved effectively, with lasting solutions that don't require ongoing senior intervention

Executive Partnership

  • A true partnership with the CPO—contributes to enterprise people strategy, not just executes on it
  • Ability to represent the CPO and the HR function credibly in executive forums, board contexts, or external settings
  • Company strategy shaped through active contribution, with people perspective integrated into all major business decisions
  • Cross-functional executive relationships at the C-suite level that drive company-level outcomes—not just coordination, but genuine strategic partnership with the CFO, CTO, and business unit leaders

Developing Executive Leaders

  • Directors (E1s) who are growing toward Senior Director or CPO-level capability through deliberate development and increasing enterprise exposure
  • An executive leadership culture within HR that models excellence for the broader organization
  • Succession planning at the executive level—the HR function isn't dependent on any single leader
  • A leadership bench that creates optionality for the company's future people leadership needs

Organizational Transformation

  • The HR function positioned as a driver of organizational transformation, not just a support function responding to business needs
  • Enterprise-wide change initiatives led effectively—cultural shifts, organizational redesigns, and workforce transformations that deliver lasting results
  • People practices that evolve ahead of business needs, positioning the company to execute on strategy without people capability becoming a bottleneck
  • Board and investor confidence in the company's people strategy, talent pipeline, and organizational health

Enterprise People Strategy

  • People strategy that positions the company competitively over 2-5 year horizons—anticipating workforce trends, talent market shifts, and evolving employee expectations
  • Major people investments that prove sound over time—leadership development programs, compensation philosophy, employer brand, and organizational design decisions that build cumulative advantage
  • People risk managed at the organizational level—understood, mitigated, and communicated appropriately to the executive team and board
  • Innovation balanced with consistency—the function can evolve practices without disrupting organizational stability

External Presence

  • The company's people capabilities represented credibly to external audiences—candidates, customers, partners, investors, and industry
  • Thought leadership that builds the company's employer brand and reputation as a destination for exceptional talent
  • Industry relationships that create strategic value—benchmarking networks, HR leadership communities, and talent market intelligence
  • Contributions to the broader HR community that reflect well on the organization and attract talent to the function

Required Skills

Executive Leadership

  • General management perspective—optimizes for organizational outcomes, not domain or functional outcomes
  • Executive partnership—operates as a peer to other C-suite executives, not a functional leader reporting up
  • Strategic thinking with 2-5 year horizons; shapes company direction through people strategy, not just HR direction
  • Board-level communication—can represent people strategy, talent metrics, and organizational health credibly to board and investors

Organizational Leadership

  • Developing executives—grows Directors toward Senior Director and CPO-level capability
  • Cross-domain coordination—ensures all HR domains work together to deliver a coherent employee experience
  • Organizational design at scale—structures the HR function and influences company-wide organizational design for effectiveness
  • Culture leadership—shapes how the HR function operates and influences how the broader organization embodies its values

HR Domain Leadership

  • Enterprise-wide people strategy—understands how all HR domains create value individually and how they compound when integrated
  • Ability to evaluate major people investments, program strategies, and organizational bets at scale
  • Industry perspective—understands workforce trends, competitive talent dynamics, and where the profession of HR is heading
  • Credibility with senior business leaders as a strategic partner with sound judgment on complex people and organizational matters

External Leadership

  • Executive presence with external audiences—candidates, customers, partners, investors, and industry peers
  • Thought leadership capability—can represent the company's people story and employer brand compellingly
  • Relationship building at senior levels—creates strategic value through external network and industry involvement

Core Competencies

  • Systems thinking at organizational level—understands how people programs, business processes, and culture interact, and creates leverage through integration
  • Influence across executives—builds alignment among C-suite peers with different priorities and perspectives on people matters
  • Judgment at high stakes—makes sound decisions with significant consequences and incomplete information, particularly in sensitive people situations
  • Resilience under pressure—maintains effectiveness and supports the organization through significant challenges including workforce reductions, cultural crises, and organizational upheaval

Required Experience

Years of Experience

  • 15-20+ years of professional experience in Human Resources
  • 8-12+ years in senior HR leadership, including meaningful time at Director (E1) level or equivalent

Demonstrated Track Record

  • Led HR organizations of significant scale supporting 500+ employees across multiple domains or business units
  • Developed Director-level HR leaders who became effective executives
  • Delivered strategic people outcomes over multi-year timeframes with measurable business impact
  • Successfully led major organizational transformations—significant growth, cultural change, M&A integration, or restructuring
  • Built and maintained HR organizational excellence over extended periods, not just short-term performance

Executive Experience

  • Operated as a senior member of HR leadership, partnering closely with a CPO, CHRO, or equivalent
  • Experience representing people strategy at board level or with investors (strongly preferred)
  • Track record of cross-functional executive partnership driving company-level outcomes, particularly with Finance, Legal, and Operations
  • Experience deputizing for or partnering closely with a CPO or CHRO

HR Domain Background

  • Broad expertise spanning multiple HR disciplines—able to set strategic direction across talent acquisition, development, compensation, employee relations, and people operations
  • Experience designing enterprise-wide people strategies that scaled across geographies, business units, or stages of company growth
  • Strong command of people analytics, workforce planning, and the use of data to drive executive-level people decisions
  • Understanding of global employment practices, regulatory complexity, and compliance across jurisdictions (for companies with global operations)
