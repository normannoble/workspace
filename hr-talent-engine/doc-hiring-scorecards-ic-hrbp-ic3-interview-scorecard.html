Level Context: What IC3 Looks Like

IC3 is a self-directed strategic advisor who owns the people strategy for assigned business units, builds deep trust with business leaders, and serves as the connective tissue between the business and HR Centers of Excellence. Evaluate for:

  • Strategic partnership — Business leaders actively seek their counsel on organizational decisions, not just when problems arise
  • Talent management ownership — Drives talent review, succession planning, and manager development independently
  • Employee relations judgment — Handles complex ER situations with appropriate urgency, discretion, and fairness
  • Organizational influence — Shapes how the business thinks about people decisions through credibility and expertise

Differentiation from IC2: IC3 owns the people strategy independently and advises leaders; IC2 supports a senior HRBP on operational tasks. Differentiation from IC4: IC3 impacts assigned business units; IC4 partners with VP/SVP-level executives on enterprise-scale challenges like M&A integration and culture transformation.

Competency Assessment

1. Strategic Business Partnership

What to assess: Ability to serve as a strategic advisor to business leaders, develop actionable people strategies, drive workforce planning, and recommend organizational design improvements.

IC3 Expectations: - Business leaders actively seek the HRBP's counsel on organizational decisions including team structure, talent planning, succession, and change management - People strategies clearly aligned with business objectives and translated into actionable plans with measurable outcomes - Workforce planning that anticipates talent needs based on business roadmap, growth plans, and attrition patterns - Organizational design recommendations that improve team effectiveness, clarify accountability, and support scaling

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a time a business leader came to you proactively for advice on an organizational decision. What was the situation and how did you add value? - Describe how you developed a people strategy for a business unit. How did you connect it to business objectives and what were the outcomes? - Walk me through your approach to workforce planning. How do you anticipate talent needs rather than reacting to gaps? - Give me an example of an organizational design recommendation you made. What problem were you solving and how did you arrive at the solution?

2. Talent Management & Development

What to assess: Ability to run talent review processes, build succession plans, develop manager capability, and drive retention of top talent.

IC3 Expectations: - Talent review process that accurately identifies high performers, high potentials, and at-risk employees with development plans that are actually executed - Succession plans for critical roles with identified successors, development timelines, and contingency options - Manager capability improved over time through targeted coaching, training recommendations, and early intervention - Career development conversations and mobility opportunities that contribute to retention of top talent

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a talent review process you led. How did you ensure the outcomes were actionable and not just shelf-ware? - Describe your approach to succession planning. Give me a specific example of a succession plan you built and how it played out. - How do you develop manager capability in your client groups? Tell me about a manager you coached whose effectiveness measurably improved. - Give me an example of how you retained a high-potential employee who was at risk of leaving. What did you do differently than the manager would have done alone?

3. Employee Relations & Organizational Health

What to assess: Judgment in handling complex ER situations, ability to monitor organizational health proactively, equitable policy application, and handling difficult conversations.

IC3 Expectations: - Employee relations issues handled with appropriate urgency, discretion, and fairness—outcomes that protect both the employee and the organization - Organizational health continuously monitored through engagement data, turnover trends, skip-level conversations, and manager feedback - Consistent, equitable application of HR policies with appropriate judgment applied to context-specific situations - Difficult conversations (performance management, restructuring, terminations) handled with empathy and professionalism

Sample Questions: - Walk me through the most complex employee relations situation you've handled. What made it complex, how did you approach it, and what was the outcome? - How do you monitor organizational health in your client groups? Tell me about a time you identified and addressed an issue before it became a crisis. - Describe a time you had to apply HR policy in a situation where strict enforcement didn't feel right. How did you balance consistency with context? - Tell me about a difficult conversation you facilitated—a termination, a performance management discussion, or a restructuring communication. How did you prepare and what did you learn?

4. HR Program Execution & Feedback

What to assess: Ability to deploy enterprise HR programs effectively, channel business-specific feedback to CoEs, manage change, and collaborate with other HRBPs.

IC3 Expectations: - Enterprise HR programs effectively deployed within assigned business units with high participation and quality - Business-specific insights and feedback channeled back to HR Centers of Excellence to inform enterprise programs - Change management for HR initiatives that accounts for business unit context, timing, and leader readiness - Cross-HRBP collaboration that shares best practices and prevents inconsistent guidance across business units

Sample Questions: - Describe a time you deployed an enterprise HR program that required significant change management in your client group. How did you adapt the approach? - Tell me about feedback you channeled from the business back to an HR Center of Excellence. How did you make it constructive and what changed as a result? - How do you ensure HR program deployment feels relevant to the business rather than like compliance theater? - Give me an example of how you collaborated with other HRBPs to align your approaches across client groups.

5. Leadership Influence & Systems Thinking

What to assess: Executive presence, ability to influence without authority, coaching and facilitation skills, systems thinking, and emotional intelligence.

IC3 Expectations: - Can advise, challenge, and coach senior leaders without positional authority - Navigates organizational politics and competing priorities while maintaining credibility and trust - Systems thinking—understands how decisions in one area cascade into others (compensation, structure, hiring affect engagement, retention, performance) - Emotional intelligence and resilience—manages high-stakes conversations and maintains objectivity

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a time you had to push back on a senior leader's decision about a people matter. How did you approach it and what happened? - Describe a situation where you had to navigate competing priorities between different stakeholders. How did you maintain trust with all parties? - Give me an example of a time you connected dots across different HR areas—where a decision in one domain had implications for another. How did you manage it? - How do you maintain your own resilience and objectivity when dealing with emotionally charged organizational situations?
