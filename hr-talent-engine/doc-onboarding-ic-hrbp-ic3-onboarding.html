30-60-90 Day Onboarding: HR Business Partner (IC3)

Overview

Level: IC3 — HR Business Partner Focus: Establishing strategic partnership, understanding business units, owning people strategy for assigned client groups Manager's Role: Context provider, strategic thought partner, appropriate autonomy

IC3 HRBPs are experienced professionals expected to own the people strategy for their assigned business units and build trust with business leaders quickly. The goal is to rapidly develop deep business context, establish credibility as a strategic advisor, and begin influencing talent and organizational decisions within the first 90 days.

Days 1-30: Learn

Theme: Map the Territory

Primary Objectives: - Deeply understand assigned business units—strategy, challenges, leadership, and talent landscape - Build relationships with business leaders, HR peers, and key cross-functional partners - Assess organizational health, talent risks, and current people priorities - Understand the HR operating model, programs, and Centers of Excellence

Week 1: Strategic Orientation

Day Focus Activities
1 Welcome HR team intros, org overview, strategic priorities, systems access
2 Business Context Deep-dive on assigned business units—strategy, financial health, competitive position, key leaders
3 People Landscape Talent review data, engagement scores, attrition trends, open headcount, current ER cases
4 HR Programs Current performance cycle, compensation philosophy, engagement action plans, active initiatives
5 Stakeholder Intros Meet business unit leaders, HRBPs supporting adjacent groups, HR CoE partners

Key Meetings to Schedule: - [ ] Manager 1:1 (weekly) - [ ] 1:1 with each business unit leader in assigned groups - [ ] HR Centers of Excellence leads (recruiting, comp, L&D, HR ops) - [ ] Peer HRBPs supporting related business areas - [ ] Key talent (high performers, high potentials) in assigned groups - [ ] Previous HRBP for assigned business units (if applicable)

Weeks 2-4: Deep Immersion

Learning Goals: - [ ] Expert-level understanding of assigned business units' goals, challenges, and operational rhythms - [ ] Clear map of talent landscape—high performers, flight risks, succession gaps, capability needs - [ ] Assessment of organizational health signals—engagement data, attrition patterns, manager effectiveness - [ ] Understanding of active ER cases, pending decisions, and sensitive situations - [ ] Initial view of where people strategy can most impact business outcomes

Activities: - Conduct skip-level conversations with key employees to understand culture and team dynamics - Review recent talent reviews, calibration outcomes, and succession plans - Analyze engagement survey results and exit interview themes for assigned groups - Observe leadership team meetings to understand decision-making dynamics - Document initial assessment and emerging hypotheses about people priorities

Milestones: - [ ] Can articulate the business strategy and key challenges for each assigned unit - [ ] Talent landscape mapped with clear view of strengths, risks, and gaps - [ ] Organizational health baseline established with data-informed perspective - [ ] Key relationships initiated with business leaders and cross-functional partners - [ ] Initial people strategy hypotheses forming based on assessment

30-Day Check-in

Manager Discussion Points: - What is your assessment of the business units and their people landscape? - What opportunities and risks have you identified? - How are relationships with business leaders developing? - What context or support do you need? - Initial feedback on contributions

Success Indicators: - [ ] Clear mental model of assigned business units and their strategic context - [ ] Key relationships established with business leaders and HR partners - [ ] First contributions demonstrating IC3-level insight and judgment - [ ] Identified 2-3 high-value people strategy opportunities - [ ] Operating with appropriate ownership and initiative

Days 31-60: Contribute

Theme: Drive Impact

Primary Objectives: - Own the people strategy for assigned business units - Influence leadership decisions on talent, structure, and organizational health - Deliver high-quality support across ER, talent management, and HR program execution - Begin building reputation as a trusted strategic advisor

Weeks 5-6: Establishing Ownership

Activities: - Take full ownership of people strategy for assigned business units - Lead talent review discussions and calibration sessions with business leaders - Manage complex employee relations situations with appropriate judgment and discretion - Drive workforce planning conversations aligned with business roadmap and growth plans - Coach managers on performance management, development planning, and difficult conversations

Demonstrating Strategic Partnership: - [ ] Business leaders actively seeking your counsel on organizational decisions - [ ] People strategies aligned with business objectives and translating into actionable plans - [ ] Complex ER situations handled with appropriate urgency, fairness, and discretion - [ ] Talent review and succession discussions producing actionable development plans - [ ] Manager capability improving through targeted coaching and intervention

Weeks 7-8: Expanding Influence

Activities: - Begin influencing decisions beyond immediate client groups where relevant - Share organizational health insights and recommend proactive interventions - Collaborate with HR CoEs to tailor enterprise programs to business unit needs - Build bridges between business units for talent mobility and knowledge sharing - Contribute to HR team-wide strategy discussions and best practice sharing

Growing Impact: - [ ] Influence on talent and organizational decisions extending beyond reactive support - [ ] Organizational health monitored proactively with data-informed recommendations - [ ] Enterprise HR programs deployed effectively with business-specific adaptation - [ ] Cross-HRBP collaboration contributing to consistent, high-quality HR support - [ ] Recognized as a credible strategic partner by business leaders

60-Day Check-in

Manager Discussion Points: - How is strategic partnership with business leaders developing? - What impact have you had on talent and organizational decisions? - Where do you want to expand influence or deepen expertise? - How are you balancing strategic work with operational demands? - Feedback on leadership and judgment

Success Indicators: - [ ] Full ownership of people strategy for assigned business units - [ ] Business leaders treating HRBP as strategic advisor, not just HR support - [ ] Complex ER and talent situations managed with sound judgment - [ ] Stakeholder relationships solid across business units and HR team - [ ] Contributing at IC3 level consistently

Days 61-90: Own

Theme: Solidify as Strategic Business Partner

Primary Objectives: - Operate fully at IC3 level as trusted strategic advisor - Shape people strategy with measurable alignment to business outcomes - Demonstrate sustained impact across talent management, ER, and organizational health - Establish trajectory for continued growth and deepening influence

Weeks 9-10: Full IC3 Operation

Activities: - Own people strategy end-to-end for assigned business units - Drive organizational design and change management recommendations where needed - Lead the most complex ER and talent situations with confidence and appropriate judgment - Mentor junior HRBPs or HR team members on business partnership skills - Ensure HR programs and feedback loops are working effectively for assigned groups

IC3 Expectations Met: - [ ] Business leaders actively involve HRBP in organizational decisions beyond people-specific issues - [ ] People strategies clearly aligned with business objectives with measurable outcomes emerging - [ ] Workforce planning anticipating talent needs based on business roadmap - [ ] Talent review producing development plans that are executed, not shelved - [ ] Organizational health continuously monitored with proactive intervention

Weeks 11-12: Setting Long-Term Trajectory

Activities: - Document people strategy vision and priorities for assigned business units - Set goals for next quarter focused on deepening strategic impact - Discuss IC4 path if interested—what senior business partnership looks like - Solidify position and reputation as a trusted strategic advisor - Fully transition from "new hire" mindset

Looking Ahead: - [ ] Clear vision for people strategy in assigned business units - [ ] Development plan established for continued growth - [ ] Relationships with business leaders solidified - [ ] Known as go-to strategic advisor for assigned groups - [ ] Positioned for continued impact and influence

90-Day Review

Manager Discussion Points: - Comprehensive onboarding assessment - Strategic partnership quality and business leader feedback - ER judgment and organizational health management - Influence and leadership demonstrated - Path forward (IC3 depth vs. IC4 breadth)

Success Indicators: - [ ] Operating fully at IC3 level - [ ] Ownership of people strategy clearly established - [ ] Strategic influence demonstrated with business leaders - [ ] Strong relationships and reputation across stakeholders - [ ] Clear on continued growth path

Key Relationships to Build

Who Why When to Meet
Direct manager Alignment, feedback, growth Weekly
Business unit leaders Strategic partnership, people decisions Weekly or biweekly
Peer HRBPs Best practices, consistency, collaboration Biweekly, ongoing
HR Centers of Excellence Program coordination, enterprise alignment First month, ongoing
Key talent in client groups Pulse on culture, retention, development First month, ongoing
Finance and Talent Acquisition partners Workforce planning, headcount, budgeting First month, as needed

Common IC3 Onboarding Challenges

Challenge Solution
Business leaders not yet treating HRBP as strategic Lead with business insight; demonstrate value through quality of recommendations
Inheriting complex ER situations without full context Get thorough handoff; review documentation; don't rush to judgment
Balancing strategic work with operational demands Establish boundaries; delegate or systematize operational work where possible
Building credibility quickly with skeptical leaders Listen first; ask sharp questions; deliver early wins on things that matter to them
Navigating unfamiliar business domain Invest time understanding the business; attend operational meetings; learn the language

Manager Checklist

Pre-Arrival

  • [ ] Assigned business units identified with clear scope
  • [ ] Handoff plan from previous HRBP or interim support
  • [ ] Key business leader meetings scheduled
  • [ ] Active ER cases and sensitive situations briefed
  • [ ] Talent review data and engagement results prepared

Week 1

  • [ ] Business context deep-dive complete
  • [ ] Stakeholder introductions done
  • [ ] People landscape and organizational health data shared
  • [ ] Active cases and priorities transitioned
  • [ ] Regular cadence established

Ongoing

  • [ ] Ownership transfer progressing on schedule
  • [ ] Strategic influence opportunities provided
  • [ ] Complex ER situations supported with appropriate latitude
  • [ ] Business leader feedback solicited and shared
  • [ ] Course corrections made as needed
