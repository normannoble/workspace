Level Context: What IC3 Looks Like

IC3 is a self-directed talent acquisition professional who owns end-to-end hiring for critical roles and elevates the recruiting function through expertise, process improvement, and stakeholder partnership. Evaluate for:

  • Strategic sourcing — Builds diverse pipelines using tailored strategies for hard-to-fill and specialized roles
  • Consultative partnership — Serves as a trusted advisor to hiring managers on talent strategy, not just an order-taker
  • Mentorship — Elevates junior recruiters through coaching, calibration, and knowledge sharing
  • Process improvement — Drives accountability for interview quality and structured evaluation across panels

Differentiation from IC2: IC3 elevates the function and partners strategically; IC2 executes reliably on standard roles. Differentiation from IC4: IC3 impacts their hiring portfolio and team; IC4 shapes recruiting strategy organization-wide.

Competency Assessment

1. Hiring Effectiveness & Quality

What to assess: Ability to fill critical roles with high-quality candidates and drive structured, bias-reducing evaluation practices.

IC3 Expectations: - Critical roles filled with high-quality candidates who pass probation, receive strong early performance reviews, and remain beyond their first year - Offer acceptance rates that consistently exceed team benchmarks, reflecting strong candidate engagement throughout the process - Hiring decisions informed by structured evaluation criteria that reduce bias and improve consistency across interview panels - A managed requisition portfolio delivered within target timelines, with proactive communication when complexity requires adjusted expectations

Sample Questions: - Tell me about the most complex or critical role you have filled. What made it challenging and how did you approach it? - How do you ensure interview panels are calibrated and applying structured evaluation consistently? - Describe a time when your structured approach to evaluation changed the outcome of a hiring decision. - How do you measure the quality of your hires after they join? What signals do you track?

2. Sourcing Strategy & Pipeline Development

What to assess: Sophistication of sourcing approach, pipeline diversity, and talent market intelligence.

IC3 Expectations: - A robust, diverse candidate pipeline for recurring and hard-to-fill roles that reduces dependency on reactive sourcing - Sourcing strategies tailored to role type and market conditions—combining direct outreach, referral programs, events, and partnerships - Talent market intelligence shared proactively with hiring managers and leadership, informing workforce planning and compensation decisions - Candidate relationship management that maintains engagement with high-potential prospects even when no immediate role is available

Sample Questions: - Walk me through how you build a sourcing strategy for a specialized or hard-to-fill role. What factors do you consider? - How do you build and maintain a pipeline of passive candidates for roles that recur frequently? - Tell me about a time your talent market intelligence directly influenced a hiring manager's or leader's decision. - How do you approach diversity in your sourcing strategy? Give me a specific example.

3. Stakeholder Partnership & Influence

What to assess: Ability to serve as a strategic advisor to hiring managers and influence role design, leveling, and process quality.

IC3 Expectations: - Hiring managers who view their recruiter as a strategic partner and actively seek input on role design, leveling, and interview strategy - Intake processes that result in well-calibrated job requirements, realistic timelines, and aligned expectations - Interview panels that are trained, calibrated, and consistently applying structured evaluation—with the Senior Recruiter driving accountability - Feedback loops with hiring managers that surface what is working and what is not, leading to continuous improvement

Sample Questions: - Describe a hiring manager relationship where you moved from order-taker to strategic partner. What changed? - Tell me about a time you pushed back on a hiring manager's role requirements or expectations. What was the outcome? - How do you train and calibrate interview panels for consistency and quality? - Give me an example of a feedback loop you established that led to a meaningful improvement in hiring outcomes.

4. Candidate Experience & Employer Brand

What to assess: Quality of candidate experience across the full process and ability to position the employer brand authentically.

IC3 Expectations: - A candidate experience that is responsive, transparent, and respectful at every stage—resulting in positive feedback even from candidates not selected - Employer brand messaging that authentically represents the organization's culture, values, and opportunities - A recruiting process that moves at a competitive pace without sacrificing evaluation rigor - Declined candidates who leave the process as advocates for the organization, not detractors

Sample Questions: - How do you ensure candidates have a positive experience even when they are not selected? - Tell me about a time you lost a strong candidate to a competitor. What did you learn and what did you change? - How do you balance moving quickly with maintaining evaluation rigor? - Describe how you communicate the employer brand to candidates for different types of roles.

5. Mentorship & Function Elevation

What to assess: Impact on junior recruiters' growth, knowledge sharing, and contributions to recruiting team capability.

IC3 Expectations: - Junior recruiters accelerated through effective coaching, interview calibration, and knowledge sharing - Strong judgment in candidate evaluation—assessing beyond surface qualifications to identify potential, cultural alignment, and risk factors - Ownership mentality—takes accountability for hiring outcomes, not just activity metrics - Continuous learning orientation—stays current with recruiting trends, tools, and labor market dynamics

Sample Questions: - Tell me about someone you have mentored on the recruiting team. How did you approach their development? - How do you share sourcing techniques, market knowledge, or evaluation methods with your team? - Describe a time you took ownership of a hiring outcome that did not go well. What did you do? - What is a recruiting trend or tool you have recently adopted? How has it changed your approach?
