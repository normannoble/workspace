Progression Guide: E2 to E3

Senior Director of HR to Chief People Officer

Overview

The transition from E2 (Senior Director of HR) to E3 (Chief People Officer) represents the shift from enterprise HR leadership to ultimate accountability for the company's people strategy, organizational culture, and the health of the employee experience. This is the move from executive partnership to executive ownership--from shaping people strategy alongside the CPO to being the person the board, CEO, and company hold accountable for how the organization wins through its people.

At E2, you're a strategic partner to the CPO, leading across HR domains and representing the function externally. At E3, you serve on the executive team as a peer to the CEO, CFO, and CTO, with full ownership of the company's people vision and strategy. You set the standard for how the organization attracts, develops, and retains talent, steward its culture, and represent the people story to the board, investors, and the broader market.

This transition is rare and represents the pinnacle of the HR leadership career path. It requires not just broader scope but a fundamentally different relationship with the organization--one where you are both architect of the company's people future and steward of its current culture and organizational health.

This transition typically takes 3-5+ years at E2, and CPO positions are extremely limited. Many outstanding Senior Directors have fulfilling careers at E2--that is a significant executive achievement in its own right.

The Core Shift

E2 (Senior Director of HR) E3 (Chief People Officer)
Cross-domain HR leadership Ultimate accountability for the entire people agenda
Executive partner to the CPO Peer to CEO, CFO, CTO on the executive team
Contributes to enterprise people strategy Owns and sets the enterprise people vision and strategy
Develops Directors toward Senior Director/CPO Develops the entire executive leadership bench across the company
Regular board presence Board accountability--presents people strategy, talent risk, and culture metrics
Represents people function externally Represents the company as an employer and people-first organization
Drives organizational transformation Stewards organizational culture and designs workforce for the future
Manages people risk within the function Owns people risk, governance, and compliance across the enterprise

The fundamental question changes from "Am I shaping how the HR function succeeds?" to "Am I the person ultimately accountable for how this company wins through its people?"**

Key Capabilities to Develop

1. People Vision and Enterprise Strategy Ownership

At E2, you contribute to and help shape enterprise people strategy. At E3, you own it--setting the vision for how the company creates competitive advantage through its people and ensuring that vision translates into sustained organizational capability over 3-5+ year horizons.

What this looks like:

  • A people strategy that creates sustainable competitive advantage--the company wins in its markets in part because of how it attracts, develops, and retains talent
  • People investments prove sound over multi-year horizons--bets on leadership development, culture, compensation philosophy, and organizational design build cumulative advantage
  • Strategic alignment between people capabilities and business strategy, with talent and culture enabling company direction rather than constraining it
  • Innovation balanced with organizational stability--practices evolve to stay ahead of workforce trends while maintaining the cultural foundation that drives performance
  • The people agenda is integrated into all major company decisions, from strategy and M&A to product and market expansion

How to develop this:

  • Develop a clear, articulate point of view on how the company should win through people over the next 3-5 years
  • Study how the best CPOs connect people strategy to business outcomes and competitive positioning
  • Build deep understanding of the company's business model, economics, and competitive dynamics--not just the people dimension
  • Practice thinking in multi-year investment terms--which people bets will compound over time?
  • Seek opportunities to present your strategic thinking to the CPO and other executives, inviting challenge and refinement

2. C-Level Executive Leadership and Board Accountability

At E2, you have regular board presence and strong executive relationships. At E3, you are a full member of the executive team, accountable to the board for people strategy, organizational health, talent risk, and culture.

What this looks like:

  • You operate as a true peer to the CEO, CFO, CTO, and other executives--with full credibility and influence on company direction
  • Board presentations on people strategy, succession planning, and organizational health build confidence in the company's talent and culture
  • Investor relations are supported effectively--the people story is articulated compellingly as part of the company narrative
  • Cross-functional executive partnerships drive company outcomes, with the people function as a strategic enabler across all parts of the business
  • Company strategy is shaped through your active contribution, not just informed by your input

How to develop this:

  • Build deeper relationships with board members and practice presenting people strategy at the governance level
  • Develop investor-level communication skills--how does the people story fit into the company narrative?
  • Strengthen partnerships with fellow C-suite executives, understanding their strategic priorities and how people strategy enables them
  • Practice making company-level strategic arguments, not just people-function arguments
  • Seek exposure to M&A due diligence, fundraising, or other situations where executive-level people judgment is critical

3. Developing the Enterprise Leadership Bench

At E2, you develop Directors toward Senior Director and CPO capability. At E3, you develop the entire executive leadership bench--not just within HR, but influencing leadership development across the company.

What this looks like:

  • E1s and E2s within HR are growing toward CPO-level capability through deliberate development
  • Succession planning at all senior levels means the organization's success doesn't depend on any single leader, including the CPO
  • The HR leadership team operates with excellence and continuously develops toward greater capability
  • Your influence on leadership development extends beyond HR--you shape how the company develops leaders across all functions
  • Culture and values across the company are actively stewarded, authentically lived, and create a destination workplace

How to develop this:

  • Shift your development lens from HR leaders specifically to enterprise leadership broadly--how does the company build leaders?
  • Take ownership of succession planning conversations at the executive level
  • Build mentoring relationships with leaders outside of HR to broaden your influence on company-wide leadership development
  • Study the best practices of CPOs who are known for building exceptional leadership benches
  • Create deliberate stretch opportunities for your E1s that expose them to the full breadth of CPO-level accountability

4. External Leadership, Employer Brand, and Industry Presence

At E2, you represent the company's people capabilities externally. At E3, you are the face of the company as an employer--your presence, thought leadership, and industry relationships directly shape how the market perceives the organization.

What this looks like:

  • The company is recognized for people excellence--employer brand attracts talent, builds customer trust, and creates partnership opportunities
  • You are recognized as a people and culture leader beyond the company--your perspective is sought by the industry
  • Customer, partner, and candidate relationships are strengthened through the company's reputation for how it treats and develops its people
  • Industry relationships create strategic value--benchmarking insights, talent market intelligence, and partnerships that advance the company's people capabilities
  • Thought leadership positions the company as a voice in the industry on talent, culture, and the future of work

How to develop this:

  • Invest significantly in building your external brand and thought leadership platform
  • Represent the company in high-profile industry settings, candidate events, and media opportunities
  • Build relationships with other CPOs and senior people leaders to create a benchmarking and insight network
  • Develop a distinctive point of view on the future of work, talent strategy, or organizational culture that reflects both the company's values and your own expertise
  • Make employer brand a strategic priority, not just a marketing exercise--connect it to talent outcomes and business performance

Common Pitfalls

Operating as a super-Senior Director instead of a CPO

The CPO role isn't "Senior Director with ultimate authority." It's a qualitatively different role where you are the architect and steward of the entire people agenda. If you're still primarily focused on making HR run well rather than shaping how the company wins through people, you're not operating at E3.

Underinvesting in board and investor relationships

CPOs are accountable to the board. If you haven't developed the skills and relationships for board-level communication about people strategy, talent risk, and organizational health, you'll struggle in the transition.

Staying narrowly focused on the HR function

The CPO's scope extends beyond HR operations to company-wide culture, leadership development across all functions, organizational design, and people strategy as business strategy. If your identity is still "head of the HR function" rather than "company people leader," the shift will be difficult.

Not developing a genuine enterprise strategy perspective

CPOs must think about the business holistically. If your strategic thinking is still grounded primarily in HR frameworks rather than business strategy with a people lens, you need to broaden your perspective significantly.

Neglecting governance and risk management

At E3, you own people risk, compliance, and governance at the enterprise level. If you haven't built capability in these areas--understanding employment law across jurisdictions, managing sensitive executive situations, handling crisis situations--you'll be exposed in the role.

How to Demonstrate Readiness

Promotion to E3 requires sustained demonstration of enterprise-level impact and executive presence over 24+ months. Here's how to build your case:

Build a Track Record

  • Document enterprise-level people strategy contributions that shaped company direction, not just HR direction
  • Track executive leaders you've developed and their growth trajectory
  • Gather feedback from the CEO, board members, and fellow C-suite executives about your strategic contribution and readiness
  • Build a visible external presence that creates tangible value--employer brand impact, talent attraction, industry recognition
  • Show evidence of sound judgment on high-stakes people decisions with lasting organizational impact

Self-Assessment Questions

  1. Am I the person others look to for the company's people vision, not just HR function leadership?
  2. Can I operate as a genuine peer to the CEO, CFO, and CTO on company strategy?
  3. Am I accountable for people strategy at the board level with credibility and confidence?
  4. Am I developing the entire executive leadership bench, not just HR leaders?
  5. Do I steward organizational culture deliberately, shaping how the company lives its values?
  6. Is my external presence creating strategic value--attracting talent, building employer brand, generating industry insights?
  7. Am I making sound, high-stakes decisions on people matters that prove right over time?

If you can answer "yes" to most of these consistently over 24+ months, you may be ready for E3.

Typical Timeline

Most leaders who progress from E2 to E3 do so in 3-5+ years at E2. This reflects:

  • Building enterprise people strategy ownership that demonstrates CPO-level thinking
  • Developing board-level communication skills and relationships
  • Growing executive leaders who demonstrate your leadership development capability
  • Building significant external presence and industry recognition
  • CPO positions being extremely limited--typically one per organization

Remember: E2 represents exceptional executive success. The transition to CPO is not just about scope--it's about a fundamentally different relationship with the organization where you are ultimately accountable for how the company wins through its people. Many outstanding Senior Directors have deeply fulfilling careers at E2. Pursue E3 only if ultimate ownership of the company's people vision, board accountability, and enterprise-wide culture stewardship genuinely align with your aspirations and strengths.
