30-60-90 Day Onboarding: HR Operations Coordinator (IC1)

Overview

Level: IC1 — HR Operations Coordinator Focus: Learning HR operations fundamentals, building HRIS proficiency, establishing accuracy and reliability habits Manager's Role: Structured guide, active coach, regular feedback provider

IC1 HR Operations Coordinators are early-career professionals building their foundation in HR systems and processes. Onboarding is highly structured with clear daily expectations, frequent check-ins, and progressive responsibility. The goal is to move from guided task execution to reliable independent processing within 90 days, with a growing understanding of how each task contributes to broader HR operations.

Days 1-30: Learn

Theme: Build the Foundation

Primary Objectives: - Understand the employee lifecycle and how HR operations supports each stage - Learn the HRIS platform, core modules, and data entry standards - Build working relationships with HR team members, IT, and key stakeholders - Establish habits of accuracy, confidentiality, and proactive communication

Week 1: Welcome & Orientation

Day Focus Activities
1 Welcome Team introductions, HR orientation, workspace and tool setup, overview of HR Ops team structure and mission
2 Systems HRIS walkthrough (navigation, core modules, data entry procedures), security and access protocols
3 Process Employee lifecycle overview—from hire to separation—and how HR Ops supports each stage
4 Compliance Data privacy and confidentiality protocols, I-9 procedures, record-keeping fundamentals
5 Practice Complete first guided HRIS transaction, review standard operating procedures, recap week with manager

Key Meetings to Schedule: - [ ] Manager 1:1 (weekly) - [ ] 1:1 with each HR Ops team member - [ ] HR Business Partners overview meeting - [ ] IT/Systems team for tool access and integration context - [ ] Benefits administration team introduction - [ ] Onboarding buddy check-in (if assigned)

Weeks 2-4: Learning the Systems and Processes

Learning Goals: - [ ] Navigate the HRIS confidently—process basic transactions, enter data, run simple reports - [ ] Understand standard operating procedures for routine employee lifecycle events - [ ] Know confidentiality and data privacy requirements for handling employee information - [ ] Recognize key compliance obligations (I-9, benefits enrollment timelines, record retention) - [ ] Understand how data flows between HRIS, payroll, and benefits systems

Activities: - Process guided HRIS transactions (new hires, status changes, personal data updates) with reviewer oversight - Practice maintaining employee records using established checklists and SOPs - Handle routine HR inquiries through established service channels with guidance - Attend HR Ops team meetings and observe cross-functional process handoffs - Build a personal reference guide of systems, procedures, and key contacts

Milestones: - [ ] First HRIS transactions processed correctly with minimal corrections - [ ] Employee records maintained accurately following data hygiene standards - [ ] Can explain the employee lifecycle stages and HR Ops responsibilities at each - [ ] Comfortable with data privacy protocols and confidentiality requirements - [ ] Knows when to ask questions vs. when to escalate to senior team members

30-Day Check-in

Manager Discussion Points: - How comfortable are you with the HRIS and core processes? - What parts of the operational workflow feel clear vs. confusing? - How are relationships developing with team members and stakeholders? - What additional training or support would be helpful? - Initial feedback on accuracy, timeliness, and attention to detail

Success Indicators: - [ ] Can navigate the HRIS and complete basic transactions independently - [ ] Understands the employee lifecycle and HR Ops role at each stage - [ ] Processes routine transactions with decreasing error rates - [ ] Communicates proactively about progress, blockers, and when help is needed - [ ] Demonstrates care for data accuracy and employee confidentiality

Days 31-60: Contribute

Theme: Build Independence

Primary Objectives: - Process HRIS transactions and lifecycle events with growing independence - Maintain employee records and data hygiene consistently without reminders - Begin identifying data discrepancies and process gaps proactively - Contribute reliably to HR operations workflow and team objectives

Weeks 5-6: Owning Routine Operations

Activities: - Take primary responsibility for processing assigned transaction types independently - Maintain employee records with consistent accuracy—data current, documents filed, standards met - Handle routine employee and manager inquiries through established service channels - Process onboarding paperwork, benefits enrollment support, and offboarding checklists - Track personal accuracy metrics and work toward team standards

Demonstrating Reliable Execution: - [ ] Routine transactions processed correctly and within expected timeframes - [ ] Employee records maintained without reminders—data hygiene standards met - [ ] Sensitive information handled with appropriate care and confidentiality - [ ] Standard operating procedures followed consistently with understanding of the reasoning - [ ] Data discrepancies flagged when identified during routine tasks

Weeks 7-8: Growing Judgment

Activities: - Handle moderately complex transactions (transfers, promotions, leave entries) with guidance available - Begin resolving routine HR inquiries independently rather than escalating everything - Contribute observations about process bottlenecks or SOP improvements to the team - Support compliance documentation activities (record audits, filing reviews) - Take on a stretch assignment (e.g., help update an SOP, assist with a data audit)

Growing Impact: - [ ] Anticipating next steps in processes without being prompted - [ ] Resolving routine inquiries independently while escalating appropriately - [ ] Feedback from team members and stakeholders is positive on reliability and accuracy - [ ] Growing understanding of how individual tasks connect to broader operational outcomes - [ ] Fewer repeat questions—building on prior learning consistently

60-Day Check-in

Manager Discussion Points: - How is your confidence processing transactions independently? - Where are you still needing guidance most? - What patterns or improvement opportunities have you noticed? - How are your working relationships with the broader HR team? - Feedback on accuracy, initiative, and professional growth trajectory

Success Indicators: - [ ] Processing assigned transactions with minimal oversight - [ ] Data hygiene consistently meets team standards - [ ] Routine inquiries handled independently with appropriate escalation - [ ] Proactively flagging discrepancies and potential process issues - [ ] Incorporating feedback effectively with visible improvement

Days 61-90: Own

Theme: Establish Yourself

Primary Objectives: - Operate as a reliable, independent HR Ops contributor the team counts on - Demonstrate consistent accuracy, timeliness, and process adherence - Show growing understanding of how operations supports the broader HR function - Establish habits and reputation that position you for continued growth

Weeks 9-10: Full Independent Operation

Activities: - Manage full assigned workload of HRIS transactions and employee lifecycle events independently - Handle compliance documentation requirements (I-9 completion, benefits enrollment, record filing) reliably - Resolve routine and moderately complex inquiries with confidence - Support team data audit activities and quality assurance checks - Contribute process improvement ideas based on hands-on experience

IC1 Expectations Met: - [ ] Employee records maintained with high accuracy through careful data entry and attention to detail - [ ] HRIS transactions processed correctly and within expected timeframes consistently - [ ] Required employment documents collected, verified, and filed per regulatory requirements - [ ] Sensitive information handled with appropriate care following data privacy protocols - [ ] Positive contributions to team through reliability, curiosity, and consistent effort

Weeks 11-12: Setting Long-Term Trajectory

Activities: - Discuss development goals and areas of interest within HR operations - Explore IC2 HR Operations Specialist path if interested—what skills to develop next - Document any process observations or learnings to share with the team - Solidify relationships across the HR function - Fully transition from "new hire" mindset to established team contributor

Looking Ahead: - [ ] Clear understanding of your strengths and development areas in HR Ops - [ ] Development plan established with manager - [ ] Relationships with HR team members and stakeholders solidified - [ ] Known as a dependable, accurate coordinator - [ ] Positioned for continued growth toward IC2

90-Day Review

Manager Discussion Points: - Comprehensive onboarding assessment - Data accuracy and process reliability evaluation - Professional growth and learning behaviors observed - Path forward—deepening operational skills vs. expanding into new HR Ops areas - Recognition and development areas

Success Indicators: - [ ] Operating reliably at IC1 level with consistent accuracy and timeliness - [ ] Process ownership clearly established for assigned transaction types - [ ] Growing understanding of HR operations beyond just assigned tasks - [ ] Strong working relationships with HR team and stakeholders - [ ] Clear on continued growth path within HR operations

Key Relationships to Build

Who Why When to Meet
Direct manager Alignment, feedback, development Weekly
Senior HR Ops team members Day-to-day guidance, knowledge sharing Daily/as needed
HR Business Partners Understanding broader HR context First month
Benefits administration team Process handoffs, enrollment support First month, ongoing
IT/Systems team Tool access, troubleshooting, system support First week, as needed
Payroll team Data integration, process dependencies First month, as needed

Common IC1 Onboarding Challenges

Challenge Solution
Overwhelmed by HRIS complexity and multiple modules Focus on one module at a time; build a personal cheat sheet; ask for repeated walkthroughs
Fear of making data errors with real employee information Use checklists for every transaction; have work reviewed early; understand that errors can be corrected
Unsure when to ask vs. figure it out Default to asking early on; discuss the "ask vs. solve" threshold with manager
Difficulty understanding compliance requirements Focus on the "what to do" first, the "why" will come with experience; keep compliance reference guides handy
Managing multiple transaction types simultaneously Use a tracking system; prioritize by deadline; ask manager for guidance on sequencing

Manager Checklist

Pre-Arrival

  • [ ] HRIS access and training materials prepared
  • [ ] System accounts and tool access provisioned
  • [ ] Assigned transaction types identified for training progression
  • [ ] Onboarding buddy or mentor assigned
  • [ ] First two weeks of meetings scheduled

Week 1

  • [ ] Orientation and system setup complete
  • [ ] HRIS walkthrough and initial training done
  • [ ] Compliance and confidentiality protocols reviewed
  • [ ] First guided transaction assigned
  • [ ] Weekly 1:1 cadence established

Ongoing

  • [ ] Accuracy and timeliness tracking in place
  • [ ] Feedback delivered regularly with specific examples
  • [ ] Complexity of assignments increasing appropriately
  • [ ] Learning behaviors reinforced and encouraged
  • [ ] Course corrections made early when needed
