Level Context: What E3 Looks Like

E3 is the senior-most HR executive with ultimate accountability for people strategy, organizational culture, talent capabilities, and employee experience. Evaluate for:

  • People vision & strategy — Sets the vision for how the company wins through people over 3-5+ year horizons
  • Executive leadership — Operates as a true peer on the executive team with board-level presence
  • Organizational excellence — Builds an HR function that is itself a competitive advantage
  • Talent & culture stewardship — Shapes culture deliberately and ensures the company has the people it needs

Differentiation from E2: E3 owns the complete people vision and represents it to the board and externally; E2 leads the function day-to-day. Differentiation from CEO: E3 shapes company strategy from the people vantage point; CEO owns the overall company direction.

Competency Assessment

1. People Vision & Strategy

What to assess: Ability to set a people vision that creates competitive advantage and make investments that prove sound over 3-5+ year horizons.

E3 Expectations: - Sets a people vision and strategy that creates sustainable competitive advantage—the company wins partly because of how it attracts, develops, and retains talent - Makes people investments that prove sound over 3-5+ year horizons: leadership development, culture, compensation philosophy, and organizational design - Ensures strategic alignment between people capabilities and business strategy, with talent and culture enabling company direction - Balances innovation with organizational stability—evolves people practices while maintaining the cultural foundation that drives performance

Sample Questions: - What is your philosophy on how a company creates competitive advantage through people? How have you put this into practice? - Tell me about a people investment you made that proved sound over 3-5 years. What was your thesis and how did it play out? - How do you ensure people capabilities enable rather than constrain business strategy? Give me a concrete example. - Describe a time you evolved people practices significantly while maintaining organizational stability. How did you manage the tension?

2. Executive & Board Leadership

What to assess: C-level executive presence, board communication, investor relations, and ability to shape company strategy from the people perspective.

E3 Expectations: - Operates as a true peer on the executive team—people perspective integrated into all major company decisions - Communicates credibly at board level on people strategy, organizational health, succession planning, and talent risk management - Supports investor relations effectively—articulates the people story as part of the company narrative - Builds cross-functional executive partnerships that drive company outcomes with the people function as a strategic enabler

Sample Questions: - Tell me about your experience operating as a C-level executive peer. How did you ensure the people perspective was integrated into major company decisions? - Describe your approach to board communication on people strategy. What do you present, and how do you build board confidence? - How have you contributed to investor relations through the people and culture story? - Walk me through a time you partnered with the CFO or CTO on a major initiative. How did you create value through that partnership?

3. Organizational Excellence & Talent Stewardship

What to assess: Ability to build an HR function that is a competitive advantage, develop executive leaders, and steward culture and talent across the company.

E3 Expectations: - Builds an HR function that attracts exceptional talent, delivers reliably, and continuously raises the bar on people practices - Develops the HR leadership team (E1s, E2s, M3s) to operate with excellence and continue growing - Ensures succession planning at all senior levels—the organization's success doesn't depend on any single leader, including the CPO - Stewards culture and values that are actively lived across the company and create a destination workplace

Sample Questions: - How have you built an HR function that is itself a competitive advantage? What made it stand out? - Tell me about executive HR leaders you've developed. How did you grow their capability toward senior executive or CPO readiness? - Describe your approach to succession planning at the senior leadership level—both within HR and across the company. - How do you steward organizational culture so it remains authentic and connected to business performance as the company grows?

4. Risk Management, Governance & Crisis Leadership

What to assess: Ability to identify and manage people risks, ensure compliance, and lead through crises.

E3 Expectations: - Identifies, quantifies, and manages people risks appropriately—retention of key talent, succession gaps, compliance exposure, and organizational health - Establishes governance structures that enable agility while maintaining appropriate controls on people practices and employment law compliance - Ensures regulatory requirements are met across all jurisdictions without creating unnecessary friction - Leads effectively through crises—sensitive people situations, public scrutiny, and workforce disruptions

Sample Questions: - How do you identify and manage people risks at the organizational level? Give me a specific example of a risk you surfaced and mitigated. - Describe a crisis you led through—a sensitive people situation, public scrutiny, or workforce disruption. What was your approach? - How do you balance governance and compliance with organizational agility? Where do you draw the line? - Tell me about a workforce transformation you managed—a restructuring, reduction, or major organizational redesign. How did you approach it?

5. External Leadership & Industry Presence

What to assess: Industry recognition, thought leadership, employer brand stewardship, and ability to represent the company externally.

E3 Expectations: - Positions the company as recognized for people excellence—employer brand attracts talent and builds trust - Demonstrates thought leadership on talent, culture, and the future of work through speaking, writing, or community involvement - Strengthens customer, partner, and candidate relationships through the company's reputation for how it treats its people - Cultivates industry relationships that create strategic value—benchmarking insights, talent market intelligence, and partnerships

Sample Questions: - How have you built your company's reputation as an employer of choice? What was your most impactful initiative? - Tell me about your external presence—thought leadership, speaking, industry involvement. How has it created value for your organization? - How do you stay ahead of workforce trends and bring external insights into your people strategy? - Describe an industry relationship or network that created strategic value for your company. What did that look like?
