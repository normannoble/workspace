30-60-90 Day Onboarding: Senior HR Manager (M2)

Overview

Level: M2 — Senior HR Manager Focus: Assessing team and program health across disciplines, establishing independent leadership, developing M1 managers, building cross-functional partnerships Manager's Role: Strategic partner, providing organizational context, supporting autonomy, offering guidance on the most complex situations

M2 Senior HR Managers are experienced leaders expected to operate with independence across multiple HR disciplines. Onboarding focuses on rapidly understanding the people, programs, and partnerships they inherit—then assessing what works, what needs improvement, and where strategic alignment is needed. The goal is to establish credible leadership quickly while resisting the urge to change things before fully understanding context.

Days 1-30: Learn

Theme: Understand the Organization

Primary Objectives: - Assess team capabilities, dynamics, and health across disciplines and management layers - Understand the HR programs, processes, and cross-discipline coordination patterns in place - Build relationships with M1 managers, business leaders, and cross-functional partners - Map the strategic landscape—organizational priorities, HR function strategy, and how your teams connect to both

Week 1: Strategic Orientation

Day Focus Activities
1 Welcome Org overview, HR function strategy, executive priorities, role expectations
2 Team Structure Meet M1 managers—understand their teams, capabilities, challenges, and leadership maturity
3 Programs Review major HR programs owned by your teams, current state, and upcoming cycle deadlines
4 Stakeholders Meet senior business leaders—understand their HR satisfaction, pain points, and strategic needs
5 Cross-Function Meet Finance, Legal, IT partners—understand cross-functional dependencies and collaboration patterns

Key Meetings to Schedule: - [ ] Manager 1:1 (weekly) - [ ] 1:1 with each M1 manager (weekly) - [ ] Skip-level 1:1s with key team members - [ ] Senior business leaders across the organization - [ ] Cross-functional partners (Finance, Legal, IT) - [ ] Previous leader or interim owner (if applicable)

Weeks 2-4: Deep Immersion

Learning Goals: - [ ] Clear assessment of each M1 manager's leadership capability, development needs, and growth trajectory - [ ] Understanding of program quality, compliance posture, and employee experience across disciplines - [ ] Map of cross-discipline coordination—where handoffs work well and where they break down - [ ] Business cycle readiness assessment—upcoming performance reviews, compensation planning, hiring surges, and open enrollment - [ ] Initial view of where strategic alignment, resource allocation, or process improvements are needed

Activities: - Observe M1 managers in their team meetings and 1:1s (with permission) to assess management practices - Review HR metrics, program outcomes, and team performance data - Attend cross-functional forums to understand how HR is perceived and positioned - Study the HRIS infrastructure, analytics capabilities, and reporting used across disciplines - Document findings and initial hypotheses without rushing to conclusions

Milestones: - [ ] Management cadence established with all M1 managers - [ ] Can articulate each team's current priorities, strengths, and challenges - [ ] Understand the full portfolio of HR programs and their business impact - [ ] Business stakeholders know you and understand your role - [ ] Initial assessment of organizational health across your teams

30-Day Check-in

Manager Discussion Points: - What is your assessment of team capabilities and M1 manager readiness? - Where do you see the biggest risks and opportunities across disciplines? - What cross-discipline coordination issues have you identified? - How are business stakeholder relationships developing? - Initial feedback on contributions

Success Indicators: - [ ] Clear mental model of teams, programs, and organizational dynamics - [ ] M1 managers feel supported and know their new leader - [ ] Key business stakeholder relationships established - [ ] Identified 3-5 high-priority areas for improvement or investment - [ ] Operating with confidence and appropriate judgment

Days 31-60: Contribute

Theme: Assess and Improve

Primary Objectives: - Begin coaching M1 managers to strengthen their management practices - Drive improvements to cross-discipline coordination and program quality - Handle complex people situations independently and decisively - Establish your leadership brand with business stakeholders

Weeks 5-6: Strengthening the Foundation

Activities: - Initiate coaching conversations with M1 managers on specific development areas - Address the most pressing cross-discipline coordination gap identified in month one - Make sound people decisions independently—hiring, performance, resource allocation - Lead a business cycle or program deliverable end-to-end through your teams - Begin shaping team strategy aligned with organizational objectives

Demonstrating Independent Leadership: - [ ] M1 managers receiving targeted coaching that improves their management practice - [ ] Complex people situation handled with confidence and sound judgment - [ ] Cross-discipline coordination improved in at least one tangible area - [ ] Resource allocation decisions made that balance team capacity with business priorities - [ ] Business stakeholders experiencing improved HR partnership

Weeks 7-8: Building Organizational Strength

Activities: - Implement a process improvement that increases efficiency or program quality - Develop or refine development plans for M1 managers - Represent the HR function credibly in cross-functional leadership forums - Build stronger partnerships with Finance, Legal, and IT on shared initiatives - Begin identifying future management candidates from the IC ranks

Growing Impact: - [ ] HR teams delivering with improved consistency and coordination across disciplines - [ ] M1 managers showing growth in management capability - [ ] Business leaders increasing their trust and partnership with your teams - [ ] Cross-functional collaboration strengthening through your engagement - [ ] Management culture across your teams reflecting shared standards and expectations

60-Day Check-in

Manager Discussion Points: - How are M1 managers developing under your coaching? - What improvements have you implemented, and what impact are you seeing? - Where do you need more authority or resources? - How are cross-functional relationships progressing? - Feedback on leadership effectiveness and strategic alignment

Success Indicators: - [ ] M1 manager development actively underway with visible progress - [ ] At least one meaningful process or coordination improvement implemented - [ ] Complex people decisions made independently with sound outcomes - [ ] Business stakeholders trust your leadership and partnership - [ ] Operating across multiple disciplines with confidence and credibility

Days 61-90: Own

Theme: Lead with Confidence

Primary Objectives: - Operate fully at M2 level—independent leadership across disciplines - Shape team strategy and align resources to organizational priorities - Demonstrate sustained impact through your teams, not personal heroics - Establish trajectory for growing organizational responsibility

Weeks 9-10: Full M2 Operation

Activities: - Own team performance and program delivery across all disciplines with full accountability - Drive strategic planning for your teams aligned with the next business cycle - Manage M1 manager performance—recognize excellence, address gaps, develop potential - Lead cross-functional initiatives that improve organizational outcomes - Handle the most sensitive people situations in your scope with sound judgment

M2 Expectations Met: - [ ] HR teams consistently delivering high-quality work across disciplines - [ ] Strong team culture with high engagement and low regrettable attrition - [ ] M1 managers growing as people leaders with clear development trajectories - [ ] Significant programs owned end-to-end with measurable outcomes - [ ] Business leaders trust and want to partner with your teams

Weeks 11-12: Setting Long-Term Trajectory

Activities: - Set team strategy and objectives for the next quarter - Develop bench strength and succession plans for key roles - Discuss M3 path if interested - Solidify position and reputation as a senior HR leader - Fully transition from "new hire" mindset

Looking Ahead: - [ ] Clear strategic vision for your teams and programs - [ ] Development plan established for your own leadership growth - [ ] Relationships solidified across business leaders, cross-functional partners, and HR leadership - [ ] Known as an effective senior HR leader who develops managers and delivers results - [ ] Positioned for continued strategic impact

90-Day Review

Manager Discussion Points: - Comprehensive onboarding assessment - Team and M1 manager development evaluation - Cross-discipline leadership and program ownership observed - Path forward (deepening M2 impact vs. broadening toward M3) - Recognition and growth areas

Success Indicators: - [ ] Operating fully at M2 level with independence and confidence - [ ] Ownership clearly established across teams and programs - [ ] M1 manager development demonstrating your leadership multiplier effect - [ ] Strong reputation with business stakeholders and HR leadership - [ ] Clear on continued growth path

Key Relationships to Build

Who Why When to Meet
Direct manager Strategic alignment, organizational context, growth Weekly
M1 managers Coaching, delegation, team health Weekly
Senior business leaders Partnership, trust, HR impact Biweekly or monthly
Cross-functional partners (Finance, Legal, IT) Coordination, shared initiatives First month, ongoing
Peer M2/M3 HR leaders Shared learning, coordination, escalation Biweekly
Skip-level team members Pulse on team health, emerging talent Monthly or as needed

Common M2 Onboarding Challenges

Challenge Solution
Inheriting M1 managers with uneven capability Assess honestly; invest in development; set clear expectations with patience
Cross-discipline coordination gaps Identify the highest-impact handoff to fix first; build systems rather than relying on heroics
Stepping in too much rather than developing managers Coach M1s through problems rather than solving for them; track your delegation patterns
Business stakeholders skeptical of new HR leadership Listen first; deliver quick wins; demonstrate strategic thinking alongside operational reliability
Balancing breadth across disciplines with depth in any one Leverage M1 managers for discipline depth; focus on integration and coordination as your value-add

  • [ ] Key business stakeholder meetings scheduled

  • [ ] Strategic context and expectations documented

Week 1

  • [ ] M1 manager introductions and 1:1s completed
  • [ ] Business stakeholder introductions done

  • [ ] Management cadence established
  • [ ] Cross-functional partner introductions completed

Ongoing

  • [ ] Leadership independence growing—handling more without escalation
  • [ ] M1 manager development progressing
  • [ ] Cross-discipline coordination improving
  • [ ] Business stakeholder trust building
  • [ ] Course corrections made as needed
