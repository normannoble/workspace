Level Context: What IC3 Looks Like

IC3 is a self-directed HR operations professional who owns critical HR systems, processes, and compliance programs while driving improvements that increase efficiency, accuracy, and employee experience. Evaluate for:

  • Systems expertise — Deep proficiency in HRIS administration, configuration, and data architecture
  • Process design — Drives standardization and workflow improvements that measurably reduce errors and cycle times
  • Compliance ownership — Proactively manages compliance programs and maintains audit readiness as a continuous state
  • Cross-functional influence — Serves as a trusted advisor on HR systems and processes whose input is sought when new programs are designed

Differentiation from IC2: IC3 owns critical systems and drives process improvement at scale; IC2 executes reliably across assigned areas. Differentiation from IC4: IC3 impacts their operational domain and team; IC4 shapes HR technology strategy and the operating model organization-wide.

Competency Assessment

1. HR Systems & Data Architecture

What to assess: Depth of HRIS expertise, system integration knowledge, and ability to ensure data serves as a trusted foundation for decisions.

IC3 Expectations: - HRIS and HR technology platforms accurately configured, well-maintained, and trusted as the system of record for employee data - Employee data that is consistently accurate, complete, and audit-ready—with discrepancies identified and resolved proactively - System integrations between HRIS, payroll, benefits, and other platforms that function reliably, with exceptions handled promptly - Reporting and analytics capabilities that enable HR leadership to make data-informed decisions about workforce trends and program effectiveness

Sample Questions: - Walk me through how you ensure data integrity across integrated HR systems (HRIS, payroll, benefits). What is your approach when integrations break? - Tell me about a reporting or analytics capability you built or improved. How did it change decision-making for HR leadership? - Describe a complex HRIS configuration you managed. What trade-offs did you consider? - How do you maintain audit-ready data as a continuous state rather than scrambling before audits?

2. Process Design & Operational Efficiency

What to assess: Ability to standardize, document, and improve HR processes while reducing errors, manual effort, and key-person dependencies.

IC3 Expectations: - HR processes (onboarding, offboarding, transfers, promotions, leave management) documented, standardized, and consistently executed - Workflow improvements that measurably reduce cycle times, eliminate redundant steps, or improve employee experience without compromising compliance - Self-service capabilities and knowledge resources that empower employees and managers to resolve routine HR questions independently - Operational playbooks and runbooks that enable consistent execution across the HR Ops team, reducing key-person dependencies

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a significant process improvement you designed and implemented. What was the measurable impact? - How do you approach standardizing a process that is currently handled inconsistently across different teams or locations? - Describe a self-service capability or knowledge resource you created. How did it affect ticket volume or employee satisfaction? - How do you build playbooks that reduce key-person dependencies without sacrificing quality?

3. Compliance & Risk Management

What to assess: Proactive compliance posture, audit readiness, and ability to translate regulatory requirements into practical operational processes.

IC3 Expectations: - Employment compliance programs (I-9, EEO, FLSA, ADA, state/local requirements) that are current, well-documented, and pass audits without material findings - Policy changes and regulatory updates identified, assessed for impact, and implemented across systems and processes before compliance deadlines - Employee records and documentation practices that meet legal retention requirements and can withstand scrutiny - A proactive compliance posture—potential risks identified and mitigated before they become violations or liabilities

Sample Questions: - Describe how you manage compliance programs across multiple regulatory requirements. How do you stay ahead of changes? - Tell me about an audit you prepared for. What was your approach, and what was the outcome? - How do you translate a new regulatory requirement into changes across HR systems and processes? Walk me through a specific example. - Describe a compliance risk you identified proactively. What did you do to mitigate it?

4. Stakeholder Partnership & Cross-Functional Collaboration

What to assess: Ability to partner with HRBPs, CoEs, IT, Finance, and Legal, and reputation as a trusted advisor on HR systems and processes.

IC3 Expectations: - HR Business Partners and Centers of Excellence teams that view HR Ops as a reliable, responsive partner—not a bottleneck - Employee inquiries and HR service requests resolved accurately and within SLA, with clear communication when issues require escalation - Cross-functional partnerships with IT, Finance, Legal, and Facilities that result in well-integrated processes and smooth handoffs - A reputation as a trusted advisor on HR systems and processes whose input is sought when new programs or tools are designed

Sample Questions: - Describe a cross-functional project you led or contributed to significantly (with IT, Finance, or Legal). What was the outcome? - How do you ensure HR Ops is seen as a partner rather than a bottleneck by HRBPs and other HR teams? - Tell me about a time your input on HR systems or processes significantly improved a program or initiative designed by another team. - How do you manage SLAs and service expectations when volume spikes or resources are constrained?

5. Mentorship & Operational Leadership

What to assess: Impact on junior team members' growth, knowledge sharing practices, and continuous learning orientation.

IC3 Expectations: - Junior team members accelerated through effective training, documentation, and knowledge sharing - Strong analytical and problem-solving skills with ability to diagnose system issues, identify root causes, and implement sustainable fixes - Detail orientation combined with systems thinking—managing precision work while understanding how processes fit into the broader HR operating model - Continuous learning orientation—staying current with HR technology trends, compliance changes, and operational best practices

Sample Questions: - Tell me about a team member you trained or mentored in HR operations. How did you approach their development? - Describe a complex system issue you diagnosed and resolved. What was your troubleshooting process? - How do you balance attention to detail on individual transactions with big-picture thinking about the HR operating model? - What HR technology trend or compliance change are you currently learning about? How are you applying it?
