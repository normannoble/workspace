30-60-90 Day Onboarding: Senior Director of HR (E2)

Overview

Level: E2 — Senior Director of HR Focus: Assessing enterprise-wide HR effectiveness, building executive partnerships at the C-suite level, developing Directors into future executives, shaping organizational transformation through people Manager's Role: Executive partner (typically CPO/CHRO), providing enterprise context and board-level access, co-designing strategy, offering counsel on the most consequential organizational decisions

E2 Senior Directors of HR operate across multiple HR domains or own day-to-day leadership of the entire function. Onboarding focuses on understanding how all of HR works together as an integrated system, assessing executive leadership bench strength, building C-suite partnerships, and developing a strategic perspective that positions the company's people capabilities over 2-5 year horizons. The goal is to establish executive credibility rapidly and begin shaping enterprise people strategy alongside the CPO.

Days 1-30: Learn

Theme: Assess the Enterprise Landscape

Primary Objectives: - Assess how the entire HR function operates as a system—coordination, strategic coherence, and organizational effectiveness across all domains - Build C-suite executive partnerships and understand company strategy at the deepest level - Evaluate Director (E1) leadership capability and executive bench strength - Understand board expectations, investor perspective, and external positioning on people matters

Week 1: Enterprise Immersion

Day Focus Activities
1 Welcome Executive team immersion, company strategy deep-dive, board context and governance overview
2 HR Function Review Assess the full HR portfolio—all domains, leadership, metrics, strategic alignment, and integration
3 Executive Leadership Meet E1 Directors—evaluate strategic capability, development needs, and executive readiness
4 C-Suite Partnerships Meet CEO, CFO, CTO, COO—understand strategic priorities, people expectations, and partnership dynamics
5 Strategic Alignment Deep-dive with CPO on enterprise people strategy, organizational priorities, and expectations for the E2 role

Key Meetings to Schedule: - [ ] CPO/CHRO 1:1 (weekly) - [ ] 1:1 with each E1 Director - [ ] Skip-level meetings with M3 Heads of HR - [ ] CEO (company strategy, culture, organizational health) - [ ] CFO (financial context, people investment ROI, workforce planning) - [ ] CTO/COO (cross-functional strategy, organizational alignment) - [ ] General Counsel (compliance landscape, employment law, risk) - [ ] Board members or committee chairs (if applicable and appropriate)

Weeks 2-4: Strategic Assessment

Learning Goals: - [ ] Comprehensive assessment of cross-domain HR effectiveness—where the function operates as a system and where domains are disconnected - [ ] Clear view of E1 Director capability, executive development trajectories, and succession strength - [ ] Understanding of how enterprise people strategy aligns (or doesn't) with company strategic direction - [ ] Assessment of the company's external people positioning—employer brand, thought leadership, industry reputation - [ ] Map of board expectations, investor perspective, and executive team dynamics on people matters

Activities: - Review enterprise HR metrics, talent analytics, engagement data, and organizational health indicators - Assess strategic coherence across domains—do hiring, development, compensation, and culture tell a consistent story? - Evaluate the HR function's scalability and readiness for the company's next stage of growth - Understand the competitive talent landscape and how the company's people practices compare to market - Develop initial strategic assessment of enterprise people capabilities and gaps

Milestones: - [ ] Leadership cadence established with all E1 Directors - [ ] Can articulate enterprise HR strengths, gaps, and strategic opportunities with board-level fluency - [ ] C-suite relationships initiated—viewed as a credible executive addition - [ ] Understanding of company financial position, competitive dynamics, and strategic trajectory - [ ] Initial enterprise assessment developed (for CPO discussion and strategic planning)

30-Day Check-in

Manager Discussion Points: - What is your assessment of the HR function's enterprise effectiveness? - Where does strategic coherence exist across domains, and where does it break down? - How do you evaluate the executive bench—E1 Directors, M3 leaders, and succession depth? - What C-suite partnerships need deepening? - Initial feedback on executive presence and strategic contributions

Success Indicators: - [ ] Enterprise-wide understanding of HR function capabilities, integration, and strategic alignment - [ ] E1 Directors trust and see value in the new senior executive - [ ] C-suite relationships established with credibility and strategic engagement - [ ] Identified the highest-priority enterprise improvements and strategic investments - [ ] Operating with enterprise executive perspective and sound judgment

Days 31-60: Contribute

Theme: Shape Direction

Primary Objectives: - Begin shaping enterprise people strategy alongside the CPO - Develop E1 Directors toward greater executive capability - Drive cross-domain coordination and strategic coherence improvements - Build C-suite partnerships that position HR as a driver of organizational transformation

Weeks 5-6: Shaping Enterprise Strategy

Activities: - Co-develop enterprise people strategy with the CPO, contributing strategic perspective and domain integration insights - Initiate executive development plans for E1 Directors focused on enterprise thinking, not just domain excellence - Make consequential decisions about cross-domain priorities, resource allocation, and organizational design - Drive resolution of the most significant cross-domain coordination gap - Represent the HR function in executive forums and company strategic discussions

Demonstrating Executive Partnership: - [ ] Enterprise people strategy taking shape with genuine E2 contribution, not just CPO execution - [ ] E1 Directors receiving coaching that builds enterprise perspective and executive judgment - [ ] Cross-domain coordination improved in ways that create a more coherent employee experience - [ ] C-suite partnerships producing strategic alignment on people investments and organizational priorities - [ ] HR function gaining momentum as a driver of organizational transformation, not just operational support

Weeks 7-8: Driving Organizational Impact

Activities: - Lead an enterprise-level initiative that demonstrates HR's strategic impact on business outcomes - Build board-level communication capability on people strategy, talent metrics, and organizational health - Strengthen executive alignment on major people investments and organizational bets - Develop the executive leadership culture within HR that models excellence for the broader organization - Begin shaping the company's external people narrative and employer brand positioning

Growing Impact: - [ ] Enterprise people strategy gaining executive team alignment and board-level visibility - [ ] E1 Directors growing in executive capability and enterprise perspective - [ ] Cross-domain integration producing measurably better organizational outcomes - [ ] C-suite partnerships deepening from coordination to genuine strategic partnership - [ ] HR positioned increasingly as a competitive advantage and transformation driver

60-Day Check-in

Manager Discussion Points: - How is enterprise people strategy shaping, and how are you contributing as a strategic partner? - What progress are E1 Directors making in their executive development? - How are C-suite partnerships evolving? - Where do you need more authority, organizational access, or board exposure? - Feedback on executive partnership quality and organizational impact

Success Indicators: - [ ] Enterprise people strategy being shaped with genuine E2 strategic contribution - [ ] E1 Director development underway with visible executive growth trajectories - [ ] Cross-domain coordination improving with measurable organizational impact - [ ] C-suite partnerships productive and creating strategic value - [ ] Operating as a true executive partner, not a senior functional leader

Days 61-90: Own

Theme: Drive Strategic Impact

Primary Objectives: - Operate fully at E2 level—enterprise HR leadership with strategic authority - Partner with the CPO to drive organizational transformation through people - Demonstrate sustained strategic impact across the HR function and the broader business - Establish long-term trajectory for enterprise people leadership

Weeks 9-10: Full E2 Operation

Activities: - Own cross-domain HR effectiveness with full accountability for coordination, prioritization, and resource optimization - Drive enterprise strategic planning with 2-5 year people perspective - Manage the executive leadership team—develop E1 Directors, build succession depth, shape leadership culture - Lead organizational transformation initiatives with enterprise scope - Represent the CPO and the HR function credibly in executive forums and external settings

E2 Expectations Met: - [ ] Multiple HR domains working together effectively, delivering a coherent employee experience - [ ] Strategic coherence across all people programs—hiring, development, compensation, and culture aligned - [ ] E1 Directors growing toward Senior Director or CPO-level capability through deliberate development - [ ] Company strategy shaped through active people-informed contribution at the executive level - [ ] HR function positioned as a driver of organizational transformation, not just a support function

Weeks 11-12: Setting Long-Term Trajectory

Activities: - Develop enterprise people strategy roadmap for the next 2-5 years - Establish executive succession plans across the HR function - Discuss CPO development path with your manager - Solidify position and reputation as a senior executive leader - Fully transition from "new hire" mindset

Looking Ahead: - [ ] Clear enterprise people vision with executive team and board alignment - [ ] Development plan established for your own executive growth toward CPO-level capability - [ ] Relationships solidified across the C-suite, board, and external networks - [ ] Known as a strategic executive who drives organizational transformation through people - [ ] Positioned for continued enterprise impact

90-Day Review

Manager Discussion Points: - Comprehensive onboarding assessment - Enterprise HR leadership and cross-domain effectiveness evaluation - Executive development and bench strength assessment - C-suite influence and organizational transformation impact observed - Path forward (deepening E2 enterprise impact vs. developing toward CPO) - Recognition and growth areas

Success Indicators: - [ ] Operating fully at E2 level with enterprise authority and executive credibility - [ ] Cross-domain ownership clearly established with strategic coherence and organizational impact - [ ] Executive development demonstrating enterprise multiplier effect - [ ] Strong C-suite partnerships and HR positioned as a strategic competitive advantage - [ ] Clear on continued growth path

CFO People investment ROI, workforce planning, financial alignment First month, biweekly or monthly

Common E2 Onboarding Challenges

Challenge Solution
Shift from domain excellence to enterprise-wide leadership Consciously delegate domain depth to E1 Directors; focus on integration, coherence, and enterprise strategy
Establishing executive partnership with the CPO (not just reporting to them) Bring strategic perspective proactively; demonstrate judgment that adds value beyond execution
E1 Directors with uneven executive readiness Assess honestly; invest in development for high-potential executives; address succession gaps directly
C-suite peers unsure of HR's strategic value at this level Lead with business outcomes; demonstrate enterprise thinking; build credibility through strategic contributions, not HR language
Board-level exposure without established credibility Prepare thoroughly; focus on business impact and talent risk language; build confidence through CPO partnership first
Balancing transformation ambition with organizational stability Sequence changes with care; protect what works while evolving what needs to change; build organizational capacity for change
